# Statistics can audit chat IQ, not rescue it

> **Update (2026-11-19).** This report's first correction has been acted on. The harness now uses t-intervals over
> personas, and the contested probes were run on 8 more personas. With 12 personas, Qwen's non-native English penalty is
> **−3.9 [−5.5, −2.4]** and its African American English penalty **−3.7 [−5.3, −2.2]**: both exclude zero, and the new
> personas replicate the original estimate. Its survival under the ignore-surface instruction still rests on the
> original 4 personas.

The newest statistical research (2023–2026) gives an IQ-from-chat project a strong toolkit for honesty and auditing, and nothing that creates information the chats do not hold. With a small random sample of people who also take a real test, prediction-powered inference (PPI++) and design-based supervised learning (DSL) give confidence intervals for population means and group gaps that stay valid however biased the LLM is. Conformal prediction gives per-person IQ ranges with guaranteed coverage. But the label savings scale as 1/(1−ρ²), only **1.33× at ρ = .5**, and an honest 90% range at that validity is still about **±21 IQ points**. Psychometrics (many-facet Rasch and hierarchical rater models, generalizability theory, DIF) and causal inference (controlled direct effects, counterfactual invariance, matched guise) give the iqaudit harness's paired shifts formal names, standard errors and a reporting grammar, and both treat its content-fixed design as the strongest available test of invariance. The debiasing tools (length-style regression adjustment, causal reward-model penalties, LEACE concept erasure, rewriting before scoring) each work only inside a narrow, stated guarantee. Applied to the harness's own free-run data, this literature forces three corrections. Qwen's −4 non-native penalty, the centerpiece of the covert-bias finding, does not exclude zero under a small-sample t-interval over its four personas (**−9.3 to +1.7**). Its flattering-prompt result cannot be read without a selection model, because Qwen declined 8 of 12 sound chats (bounds of **−1 to +8 points**). And Qwen's self-reported ranges, about **20 points wide**, are roughly half the width of the best-case honest interval. No published study applies PPI, DSL or conformal methods to LLM-inferred IQ or EQ, and none fits a rater model with a dialect facet on fixed content, so the harness occupies an open niche. The highest-value adoptions are cheap: more personas with honest small-sample intervals, an overt-versus-covert difference-in-differences table, a double-rewrite test of "canonicalize then score," and refusal-aware sycophancy reporting.

*Numbers from the harness come from the free run (qwen3.7-plus, 4 synthetic personas, 3 repeats; gemini-3.5-flash partial). Calculations marked "my calculation" were made for this report from `iq-audit-harness/runs/free/results.jsonl`. Where the companion analysis (`reports/IQ from chat mathematical analysis.md`) already derives a result, this report cites its section instead of repeating it.*

## A few test scores buy valid intervals, not sharper individuals

Every method in this family starts from the same split. An LLM rates the chats of N people, giving f(X), and a random n of them also have a real test score Y. The original prediction-powered estimator of a mean is θ̂ = (1/N)Σf(X̃) − (1/n)Σ(f(X) − Y): the LLM's average over everyone, minus its average error (the "rectifier") on the tested few. Its interval is asymptotically valid **for any predictor, however biased** ([Angelopoulos et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09633)), and the same rectification extends to quantiles and regression coefficients. PPI++ puts a weight on the prediction term, λ̂ = Cov(Y,f)/((1+n/N)·Var(f)), which makes the estimator asymptotically never worse than using the tested sample alone ([Angelopoulos, Duchi & Zrnic 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.01453)). With power tuning and a large unlabeled pool, n tested people are worth n/(1−ρ²), where ρ is the correlation between the LLM score and the test score: **1.10× at ρ = .3, 1.33× at .5, 1.96× at .7**, and 5.26× only at .9. These are the same factors the companion analysis gives (§9). Power tuning is not optional here. Qwen's outputs sit at 114.5 on the harness's ordinary-reasoning chats and move a full SD on one flawed message (companion §4), so plain PPI with λ = 1 on the raw scale can do worse than ignoring the LLM.

Most 2025–2026 work sharpened the limits. An exact finite-sample analysis shows PPI++ beats gold-only estimation only when ρ exceeds about 1/√(n−2), **0.33 with 11 tested people and 0.15 with 50** ([Mani et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20178)). When a judge is no more accurate than what it judges, no debiasing method can cut the required labels by more than half, and savings beyond 2× were rare in practice ([Dorner, Nastl & Hardt 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.13341)). The Mixed Subjects Design authors, who extended PPI with a power analysis for LLM-simulated survey respondents, saw effective-sample-size gains of only **up to about 15%** ([Broska, Howes & van Loon 2025](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/somere/v54y2025i3p1074-1109.html); [commentary](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/2025/12/19/validating-language-models-as-study-participants-how-its-being-done-why-it-fails-and-what-works-instead/)). Two more limits bind this project. ρ is the correlation with the *observed* test score, so an unreliable short test and a single noisy LLM call both lower it. And PPI estimates population quantities (mean ability, quantiles, group gaps), never one person's IQ. In a validation study, the LLM ratings' value is breadth, a score for everyone, not a large cut in how many people must sit a test.

These estimators earn their place by measuring bias against ground truth. Regress Y on a native/non-native indicator using the PPI++ rectified gradient and you get a valid estimate of the true group gap. Comparing it with the raw gap in LLM scores measures how much of the LLM's gap is bias, the observational counterpart of the harness's controlled shifts, and the harness says in advance where to look. Stratified PPI runs the correction within strata, allocates labels across them, and gives substantially tighter intervals when the autorater's accuracy differs by segment ([Fisch et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.04291)). The harness has already found the strata where Qwen's error differs: non-native, dialect, jargon-heavy, paste-heavy and phone-typed text.

Design-based supervised learning makes the sampling design itself the source of validity. The researcher records each person's probability π of being tested and builds the pseudo-outcome Ỹ = ĝ + (R/π)(Y − ĝ), which keeps inference valid even when the LLM labels are arbitrarily biased. Naive use of LLM labels was badly biased even at 80–90% accuracy ([Egami et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.04746)). Confidence-driven inference sends human labels to the cases where the LLM is least confident and cut annotations by more than 25% on text-labeling tasks ([Gligorić et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.15204)). The design rule is to fix and log π before anyone is tested, and to oversample the groups where the harness found sensitivity, which is both efficient and valid.

Three traps sit downstream. Using a chat-inferred IQ as a *predictor* of some outcome biases the coefficient, and the main problem is the bias, not wrong standard errors ([Battaglia et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15585)). The classical fix, SIMEX, assumes the error is the same in every group, which the harness has shown is false. Without any validation sample, prompt and model choices swing LLM-based estimates in magnitude, sign and significance ([Ludwig, Mullainathan & Rambachan 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.07031)). And when chats rather than people are the unit, standard PPI++ intervals can badly under-cover (below 60% in one application), which cluster-robust versions fix ([Broska & Howes 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09601)). The harness's own bootstrap resamples personas before repeats for the same reason.

Conformal prediction supplies the per-person piece. Split conformal regression holds out n_cal tested people and takes the ⌈(n_cal+1)(1−α)⌉-th smallest residual as the interval's half-width. For a new person drawn like the calibration people, coverage is guaranteed to lie between 1−α and 1−α+1/(n_cal+1), with no model of the LLM's errors ([Lei et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.04173)). Conformalized quantile regression lets the width vary from chat to chat ([Romano, Patterson & Candès 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03222)). The guarantee is marginal, and its realized value is random: for a given calibration set, coverage follows a Beta distribution ([Angelopoulos & Bates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511)). So a "90%" range calibrated on **50 people has a coverage SD of 0.041 and realistically lands anywhere from 82% to 96%**, and 200 people halve that spread. A 90% interval is infinite until at least 9 people are in the calibration set.

Width follows validity. Under a Gaussian approximation the 90% half-width is 1.645·15·√(1−ρ²): **±21 points at ρ = .5 and ±18 at .7**, against ±25 with no information at all. Marginal coverage can hide 80% coverage in one language group and 95% in another. Calibrating separately per group (Mondrian conformal), or using the conditional guarantees of [Gibbs, Cherian & Candès (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.12616) for overlapping groups such as first language × chat length, restores per-group coverage. **Equal coverage with unequal width** then becomes a fairness statistic in its own right. Conformal methods have reached LLM judges. [Sheng et al. (EMNLP 2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18658) built intervals from the judge's logits over rating tokens and added an ordinal boundary adjustment for discrete scales. Their interval midpoints matched raw scores on correlation with humans and had lower MSE. The ordinal treatment fits Qwen, whose 342 estimates took only 29 distinct values, 63% of them multiples of 5 (companion §2). Calibrated abstention generalizes the empty-log probe: Trust or Escalate certifies agreement with humans on the cases a judge chooses to answer ([Jung, Brahman & Choi 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18370)).

The harness can already test one necessary condition of honest uncertainty without any test scores. Its prompt asks for a range, and across 342 numeric replies **Qwen's stated ranges averaged 19 points wide (median 20)**; Gemini's averaged 18 (my calculation). The prompt names no coverage level. Even so, the best published text model (r ≈ .75) leaves a 95% range 38 points wide, and r = .5 leaves 50 (companion §5). Qwen's ranges are about half the width the evidence can support, around a center that is itself offset. A calibrated rater should also widen its range, and pull its estimate toward 100, as evidence thins; a truncated-log probe can check that (see the section on text below).

This family leaves a complete gap. I found no paper that applies PPI, DSL or conformal prediction to LLM-inferred personality, IQ or EQ. The closest data set, 62,090 real ChatGPT conversations from 668 people, comes with classifier accuracies and no uncertainty layer ([Cögendez et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19785)).

## Rater psychometrics shows the −4 penalty rests on three personas

Many-facet Rasch measurement (MFRM) models the log-odds of a rating category as person ability minus task difficulty minus rater severity minus a category threshold. Extra facets enter as extra subtracted terms, and rater × facet interactions measure "differential rater functioning" ([Bayesian MFRM, PMC10615980](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10615980/)). Since 2024 a small literature has fitted it to LLM essay scorers. Jiao, Song & Lee compared 10 LLMs with human experts; GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5 Pro and Claude 3.5 Sonnet showed the fewest rater effects ([2025](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2505.18486v1)). The most complete audit, a pre-registered study of 12 judges scoring 2,377 essays, found that **judge severity spanned 219 points on a 0–1000 scale**, that **every one of five model-version changes shifted severity beyond a permutation null, by up to 133 points**, and that judges correlated with humans at only **.47–.56** even though repeat calls agreed with each other (Φ ≥ .80 at two calls) ([Sunkavalli 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29517v1)).

Map the harness onto this model. Content is a facet held fixed across variants, so each variant's severity term λ_v directly estimates the construct-irrelevant leniency or severity of that surface form. Model × variant is differential rater functioning. Model version also has to be a facet. The harness already records the served model ID; what it lacks is a fixed anchor set re-run on every pass, like the "identity canaries" that caught a silently deprecated judge in Sunkavalli's study. On sound chats, Qwen and Gemini differ by about 5 points (114.5 vs about 119). That rater-severity gap is **as large as the dialect and non-native penalties being audited**, so any absolute "IQ 120" belongs to the rater as much as to the person.

The hierarchical rater model (HRM) corrects a specific error MFRM makes with repeated calls. MFRM treats every rating as independent given ability, so k calls on one chat shrink a person's standard error like 1/√k without limit. The HRM routes ratings through a latent "ideal rating" of each response, so the information about a person is bounded by the number of distinct chats, not the number of ratings. Its signal-detection form also gives each rater a precision parameter as well as a severity ([DeCarlo, Kim & Johnson 2011](https://www.tc.columbia.edu/faculty/ld208/faculty-profile/files/JEM_11.pdf)). An HRM combining several automated essay scorers matched the best single model's accuracy and **reduced differential item functioning between human and machine scoring** ([Fink et al. 2024](https://research.ou.nl/en/publications/a-hierarchical-rater-model-approach-for-integrating-automated-ess/)). That yields a testable prediction for the harness: an HRM ensemble of Qwen and Gemini should show smaller variant effects than either model alone. I found no paper that fits an HRM to LLM judges, and none that fits an MFRM with a dialect or L1 facet on content held constant. The harness design is new on both counts.

Generalizability theory (G-theory) splits score variance into facets and reports two coefficients: Eρ² for ranking people, and Φ for absolute decisions, where rater and prompt main effects count as error. "IQ 128" is an absolute claim, so Φ is the right index. Recent findings are sobering. Seven LLMs scoring earnings-call transcripts agreed at an average rank correlation of .52, **only 34% of score variance was text-level signal shared across providers**, and the choice of model changed the sign and significance of downstream regressions ([Boustanifar & Mansouri 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.31013)). A methodological paper warns that one judge under one prompt "supplies no variance component of its own," and shows that the design of the item bank alone moved KR-20 from 0.01 to 0.68 at a fixed judge error rate ([Zhu 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29709)). Human–AI composites improved reliability in AP Chinese scoring ([Song, Lee & Jiao 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.19980)), and a graded-response IRT model now separates a judge's consistency under prompt variation from its alignment with humans ([Choi et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00521)).

The companion analysis already estimated the harness's variance components for Qwen: replicate noise SD 3.0, persona content SD 4.6, persona × variant SD 1.8 (§2). G-theory adds two points.

First, in real chat logs, dialect, first-language and jargon habits are stable traits of the person. Their effect on the LLM score is therefore reliable, and it lands inside person variance, **inflating reliability while lowering validity**. Only a within-content manipulation like the harness's moves that variance into a facet that can be estimated.

Second, the standard error of a probe shift is √[(σ²_cv + σ²_e/n_r)/n_c], where n_c is the number of personas (content items) and n_r the number of repeats. Personas, not repeats, set precision. The companion analysis reached the same conclusion from power (12 personas to resolve 3-point shifts); at 12 personas the 95% interval on a shift would be about ±2.2 points.

The four-persona design has a sharper consequence than "wide intervals." With so few clusters, a percentile bootstrap is optimistic. A t-interval on the four persona-level shifts uses 3 degrees of freedom, a critical value of 3.18 instead of 1.96. On the free-run data (my calculation), the per-persona non-native shifts were +1.3, −5.7, −5.7 and −5.3. That gives **−3.8 with a t-interval of −9.3 to +1.7**, and −4.3 (−9.3 to +0.7) under the ignore-surface instruction. The other probes split by how consistent the personas are:

| Probe (Qwen, neutral question) | Mean shift | t-interval | Excludes 0? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Non-native English | −3.8 | −9.3 to +1.7 | No | One persona shows no penalty |
| Ignore-surface instruction, non-native | −4.3 | −9.3 to +0.7 | No | One persona shows no penalty |

The persona with no non-native penalty shows either non-uniform DIF (the penalty depends on content) or a content difference in that persona's rewrite. The harness's bootstrap intervals did exclude zero, as the companion analysis reported. The t-intervals say that conclusion currently rests on three personas.

DIF and invariance testing supply the right vocabulary and the observational toolkit. The harness fixes the latent trait by design, so each variant's mean shift is an anchor-free estimate of uniform DIF, and any dependence of the shift on content is non-uniform DIF. The crosswalk:

| Harness quantity | Causal estimand | Psychometric name | What to report |
|---|---|---|---|
| Variant minus base, same persona | Controlled direct effect of form, content fixed | Uniform DIF; rater leniency/severity facet λ_v | Shift in points, persona-level interval, adverse-impact ratio |
| Shift that varies by persona or reasoning quality | Form × content interaction | Non-uniform DIF; σ²_cv | Flawed × surface cells; slope on designed reasoning quality |
| Base minus flawed | Effect along the legitimate path | Construct-relevant sensitivity (discrimination) | Control effect under every framing |
| Model × variant | Effect modification by rater | Differential rater functioning | Same probe across models |
| Framing × variant (e.g. ignore-surface instruction) | Effect modification by context | Prompt-template facet, σ²_vt | Difference-in-differences of shifts |
| Repeats on one chat | None | Replicate error σ²_e | Never counted as information about a person |
| Model's level on base chats | None | Rater severity α_m | Φ alongside Eρ²; linking across models |

Two reporting lessons follow.

**Ratio statistics can flatter a fix.** The ignore-surface instruction raised the control effect from 15.1 to 22.8. The non-native shift's ratio to the control therefore fell from .25 to .19, even though the absolute penalty grew from 3.8 to 4.3. Its adverse-impact consequence (companion §6) did not change at all. Report absolute shifts beside any ratio.

**Observational studies find the same pattern on real text.** A fine-tuned Gemma-3-27B scorer, evaluated on all 12,100 TOEFL11 essays, **scored European-L1 writers above East-Asian-L1 writers within every human-assigned proficiency band** ([Gayed 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14605)). That is the observational twin of the harness's Chinese-L1 non-native probe. When the user eventually scores real logs, the deployment-time invariance test is moderated nonlinear factor analysis (MNLFA) with continuous moderators such as dialect-feature density, jargon density and paste fraction. Its regularized version lets the data choose the anchors ([penalized MNLFA, JEBS 2024](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:sae:jedbes:v:49:y:2024:i:6:p:976-1012)). The harness's controlled shifts then serve as its priors and validation targets. Throughout, content equivalence is the identifying assumption: the Chinese and non-native English texts have had native review, and the African American English guise has not.

Validity frameworks decide what counts as bias in the first place. Wallach et al. argue that GenAI evaluation usually skips the step of turning a background concept into an explicit "systematized concept" before building an instrument ([ICML 2025](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wallach25a.html)). ETS authors argue that GenAI scoring needs more validity evidence than feature-based scoring, not less ([Casabianca et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.02334)). In Kane's validity argument, the inferences form a chain, and the harness tests only the first link, scoring. Generalization needs a G-study over chats, extrapolation needs test scores, and the decision link needs DIF across groups.

The choice of construct is not academic. Suppose the construct is "reasoning the person demonstrates in their own words": then a pasted physics abstract that adds 11 points is bias. Under a purely predictive definition ("forecast this person's test score"), pasting technical text might be a legitimate correlate. A 2026 *Assessing Writing* paper raises this predictive-versus-explanatory tension for automated scoring ([2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1075293526000541)). The harness should state its construct explicitly, because its probes only make sense under the first definition. An item-response route also exists: treat each reasoning episode in a chat as an item with its own difficulty, so that test information reveals when a person's chats are too easy to place them at high ability.

Reliability then caps what any validation can show: the observed r cannot exceed √(ρ_xx·ρ_yy). Take a chat measure with reliability .80, validated against ICAR16 (ω ≈ .77 in one student sample, [Lund thesis](https://lup.lub.lu.se/student-papers/search/publication/9125503)). It cannot show more than r = .785, and a true correlation of .60 appears as .47. The companion analysis covers the Spearman-Brown ceilings on adding chats (§8).

## Invariance has a causal definition that matched pairs test directly

Each harness shift is a controlled direct effect, E[f(X(c,s))] − E[f(X(c,s₀))]: the change in score when the surface form s is set by intervention and the reasoning content c is held fixed ([Pearl 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.2300)). Because the design fixes content, the headline numbers need no mediation model. The controlled effect equals the natural direct effect only if form and content do not interact, which is one more reason to add flawed × surface cells.

Path-specific fairness asks the auditor to declare in advance which causal paths are legitimate ([Chiappa 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.08139)). Take the graph ability → reasoning → text ← surface form. The reasoning path is legitimate and every surface path is not. Pasted text is a special case: there the legitimate mediator is authorship, not the presence of sophisticated content. The broken-reasoning control is a positive control on the legitimate path, and the surface probes are negative controls whose ideal effect is zero.

Counterfactual invariance makes the target precise: a predictor is invariant if changing only the irrelevant factor never changes its output ([Veitch et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00545)). When the label causes the text, as ability causes writing, an invariant predictor must satisfy f(X) ⊥ S | Y. That is, among people with the same tested IQ, scores do not depend on dialect or first language. A validation study should test this conditional condition, not equal mean scores across groups, because in any real sample first language can correlate with an English-language test score. The harness's pairs test a stronger, individual-level version, f(X(c,s)) = f(X(c,s₀)), which is why they need no test scores.

The matched-guise probing of [Hofmann et al. (Nature 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00742) is the same design with log-probabilities in place of scores. African American and Standard American English texts with the same meaning produced conviction rates of **68.7% versus 62.1%** and death sentences of **27.7% versus 22.8%** ([methods](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00742v1)). Hofmann also reports a variant on independent natural texts that are not meaning-matched. Running both versions on the IQ question would show whether natural dialect transcripts carry a larger or smaller penalty than the hand-written guises, a direct check on guise artifacts.

RATE solves the problem that arises as soon as an LLM does the rewriting. Estimating an attribute's effect by rewriting each text once is biased, because the rewriter changes other things too; GPT-4o silently fixed typos and formatting. RATE rewrites twice and compares R(Re(Re(y,0),1)) with R(Re(y,0)), so both arms carry the same rewrite artifacts. It showed that naive estimates overstated length effects in real reward models ([Reber et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.11348v3)).

The harness's variants are hand-written, so this matters for two arms: `ai_polished`, and the companion analysis's proposed fix, canonicalize then score (§7). By RATE's logic, the fair comparison is score(canon(non-native)) against score(canon(base)), not against the raw base, so whatever polish the canonicalizer adds cancels out. And canon(flawed) must stay broken; if it doesn't, the canonicalizer is repairing reasoning.

A 2026 result suggests an in-model alternative. Forcing a judge to lock its evidence before it scores ("proof before preference") markedly improved invariance to non-evidential cues, beating structured chain-of-thought ([Tapwal, Kumar & Maple 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23970)). For the harness, that suggests an extract-then-score pipeline. One call lists the person's claims and inferences in neutral English; a second call scores that list without seeing the original text.

Regression adjustment is the cheapest debiasing tool and the easiest to misuse. Length-controlled AlpacaEval fits a logistic model with three terms (model strength, a bounded tanh length term and instruction difficulty), then predicts with the length term set to zero. That narrowed one GPT-4 model's win rate across verbosity prompts **from 22.9–64.3% to 41.9–51.6%** and raised its correlation with Chatbot Arena from .94 to .98. It also needed weak regularization of the length coefficient: without it, a deliberately truncated output's win rate rose from 3.7% to 25.9%, and with it only to 12.2% ([Dubois et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.04475v2)).

The IQ analogue fits IQ_is = α + u_i + Σβ_k·g_k(N_k,is) + ε over transcripts i and variants s. Here u_i is a transcript effect and N_k are nuisance measures such as padding tokens, paste fraction and error rate. The adjusted score is the prediction at reference nuisance levels. Because the harness identifies the β's experimentally, it avoids RATE's warning about confounded observational fits. Four limits still bind. Vocabulary is part of verbal ability, so adjusting for it removes legitimate signal; adjust for padding added with content held fixed, not for raw length. Effects interact with content, so one global coefficient mis-corrects. Adjustment fixes average scores, not any individual's, and it misses nuisances nobody measured. And the coefficients are not portable: they belong to one model, prompt and version, and the ignore-surface instruction alone moved Qwen's paste coefficient from about 5.8 to 0.7.

Training-time invariance matters only if the user trains a scorer, but the harness is unusually well placed to feed one. Causal reward models add a maximum-mean-discrepancy penalty that equalizes score distributions across bins of a spurious variable. The conditional version cut the share of prompts where all 50 sampled responses were sycophantic **from 92.67% to 19.78%**, and a concept-bias index from 18.88 to 0.52 ([Wang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.09620v2)). Counterfactual data augmentation guided by causal structure improved out-of-distribution accuracy on clinical narratives ([Feder et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.12803)). The harness's variants are ready-made augmentation pairs, and with pairs available the direct penalty E[(f(X(c,s)) − f(X(c,s₀)))²] is stronger than any penalty on distributions.

Concept erasure gives an exact guarantee with a narrow scope. LEACE finds, in closed form, the smallest least-squares edit of a representation that makes a concept linearly unrecoverable. No linear predictor can then beat a constant; equivalently, the cross-covariance between representation and concept is zero, and every linear head satisfies statistical parity ([Belrose et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03819v4)). But parity is the marginal condition, not the conditional f ⊥ S | Y that counterfactual invariance requires. The harness's matched pairs are the best case for LEACE, because content is balanced across variants, so the covariance it removes reflects only surface form. Still, the guarantee is narrower than it sounds. Groups remain nonlinearly separable after LEACE ([2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2503.20098)), proposed nonlinear guards are still disputed ([Obliviator 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.07529)), and whether erasure inside a model changes its behavior at all is an open question ([AACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aacl-short.21.pdf)). LEACE therefore fits one use: an embedding scorer with a linear head (see the section on predicting ability from text), and the head must stay linear. It cannot be applied to a closed API model. For Qwen, the activation-level route runs through an open-weight checkpoint. In LLM judges, biased inputs are displaced along low-dimensional, bias-specific directions. Steering along those directions reproduces or removes biased scoring, with effects about **10× larger than matched random directions**, and a projection onto them predicts judge failures on held-out benchmarks ([Xu et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11871)). Any erasure or steering result should pass three checks: surface-label probes at chance, a paired shift near zero, and an intact broken-reasoning control.

## Instructions remove the biases a model names, not the silent ones

The covert-versus-overt pattern is now replicated across settings. Human-feedback training made GPT models' overt statements about African Americans far more favorable (**−0.221 to 1.047**) without changing the covert stereotypes that dialect triggers ([Hofmann et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.00742v1)). Eight value-aligned models that look unbiased on standard benchmarks, GPT-4 included, showed implicit bias on an IAT-style prompt measure across more than 33,000 prompts, and that measure predicted biased decisions better than embedding-based measures ([Bai et al., PNAS 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.04105)). Explanations leave out what drove the answer: chain-of-thought rationalizes answers driven by a biasing feature without mentioning it, with accuracy falling by up to 36% ([Turpin et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.04388v2)), and reasoning models often fail to mention hints they used ([Chen et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410)).

Two findings bear directly on the non-native probe. GPT-4o, scoring essays, made larger errors on non-native writers when it correctly inferred they were non-native ([Yang et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21330)). Reward models agreed with humans about 4 percentage points less often on African American Language and steered conversations toward mainstream English ([Mire et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12858)). The counter-evidence is real: when GPT scored math responses, small changes in the number of AAVE features did not move scores, while changes to the prompt wording did ([EDM 2025](https://educationaldatamining.org/EDM2025/proceedings/2025.EDM.long-papers.195/index.html)).

The harness can formalize the pattern as a difference-in-differences. For each probe S and instruction condition I, let B_S(I) be the behavioral score shift. Let V_S(I) be the rate at which explanations mention the surface feature, minus that rate on the base text. A bias is covert when B ≠ 0 while V ≈ 0. The instruction's effects are ΔB and ΔV:

| Probe (Qwen) | Shift, plain question | Shift, ignore-surface instruction | ΔB | What the explanations did |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pasted block | +5.8 | +0.7 | −5.1 | Credited the paste as expertise ("graduate-level physics content") |
| Jargon padding | +6.0 | +3.1 | −2.9 | 92% credited vocabulary; none called it padding |
| Phone-style typos | −2.9 | −1.7 | +1.2 | 83% mentioned the typing |
| African American English | −3.8 | −2.8 | +1.0 | 75% called the dialect irrelevant to ability |
| Non-native English | −3.8 | −4.3 | −0.5 | **Mentioned the English in 100% of replies before; 0% after** |

The non-native row is the covert signature: ΔB ≈ 0 while ΔV = −100 points. The companion analysis offers a mediation reading (§7). An instruction can zero the weight on cues the model counts and names. But non-native grammar seems to lower the model's perception of the reasoning itself, a path an instruction about what to weigh cannot reach. The joint test is H₀: ΔB = 0 for the non-native probe, together with ΔV < 0, with intervals that resample personas. Given the t-intervals in the previous section, this is a strong hypothesis, not yet a result.

Two mechanisms compete to explain the penalty, and cheap probes can separate them. If grammar lowers the model's perception of clarity, canonicalizing the text with RATE's double rewrite removes the penalty. If the model instead infers "non-native speaker" and applies a stereotype, a cue intervention in the spirit of [Tapwal, Kumar & Maple (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23970) exposes it: show the base text with a one-line note that the writer is a non-native speaker. A drop in score with the grammar unchanged isolates the identity path, and Yang et al.'s finding that bias is largest where the model recognizes the writer's first language predicts it will appear. Asking "Is this writer a native English speaker?" about each transcript tests whether the penalty concentrates in the transcripts the model flags. Forced-choice probes in the style of Bai et al. ("which of these two people reasons better?", order counterbalanced, transcripts differing only in surface form) sidestep Qwen's 5-point rounding grain. And with an open-weight Qwen checkpoint, a non-native direction estimated from matched pairs would show whether the instruction changed outputs while leaving the representation untouched.

Sycophancy research has converged on three measurements. The first counts answer flips under pushback, split into progressive (incorrect to correct) and regressive (correct to incorrect): **58.19% across three models (43.52% progressive, 14.66% regressive)**, with rebuttals that cited sources producing the most regressive flips ([SycEval, Fanous et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08177v1)). The second measures preference for matching the user's views, which preference models and human raters themselves reward ([Sharma et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548)). The third is a Bayesian normative test that treats the user's opinion as evidence and measures how far the model's belief moves beyond a rational update ([Atwell et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16846)).

The Bayesian framing fits IQ estimation unusually well. A bare self-claim ("I scored 140") is weak evidence; a proctored WAIS result is strong evidence. By my calculation, a proctored score with reliability .97 has an error SD of about 2.6 points, so it should carry about 96% of the weight against chat evidence with a 13-point standard error. A rater that moves equally for "I scored 140," "my friend says I scored 140" and "my proctored WAIS says 140" is either sycophantic or under-updating, and the harness can measure both without ground truth. Mitigations with evidence behind them include synthetic training data in which the user's opinion is independent of the truth ([Wei et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03958)), Bayesian-calibrated fine-tuning (BayesSFT and BayesDPO), and the causal reward-model penalty described above.

Refusal dominates the harness's sycophancy data, and the literature rarely models refusal. Under the flattering-anchor prompt, Qwen declined **8 of 12 sound-reasoning chats but only 5 of 12 broken ones**. The four sound chats it did score averaged 117.5, against 114.5 under the neutral question (my calculation). Because refusals depend on content, the +2.3 framing shift in the report cannot be read as inflation. Lee-style trimming assumes flattery only ever adds refusals. It compares the answered cases with the top and bottom third of the neutral distribution, and **bounds the inflation on always-answered chats between −1 and +8 points**.

The control effect under flattery fell from 15.1 to 7.7, which would mean flattery halves the rater's sensitivity to reasoning. But that figure rests on three personas and selective refusals. Gemini shows the same structure in another place: under the neutral question it declined 3 of 4 broken-reasoning chats, so its +23 control effect comes from one persona. Refusal is part of the outcome. Until a selection model is fitted, the honest summary is a two-part (hurdle) report: decline rates per cell, shifts among answered cases, and bounds.

## Text carries under one bit about IQ, mostly verbal ability

The best supervised result still comes from essays, not chats. Embeddings of about 250 words written at age 11, fed to an untuned SuperLearner with nested cross-validation, predicted general ability at **holdout R² = .57 but non-verbal ability at only .37**. That roughly matched teachers (.62) and far exceeded polygenic scores (.15). Embeddings alone did almost as well as embeddings plus hundreds of hand-built features, GPT-family embeddings beat RoBERTa, and root type-token ratio was selected in 96.6% of models ([Wolfram 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12229686/)).

Zero-shot inference is weaker. GPT-4 inferred Big Five traits from Facebook posts at an average r = .31, against .37 for supervised models ([Peters & Matz 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11211928/)). Chatbot conversations gave r = .44 when the bot elicited trait-relevant talk, but **.12 with a default assistant** ([Peters, Cerf & Matz 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13052)). For personality, averaging across seven LLMs beat any single one ([Wright et al. 2026](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-025-02389-x)).

With hundreds rather than thousands of labeled people, the practical recipe is frozen embeddings reduced with PCA (human-level prediction tasks matched full performance with about 1/12 of the dimensions, [Ganesan et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.03484)), ridge or elastic net stacked with a tree ensemble, nested cross-validation, and cheap lexical-diversity and error-rate features. I found no 2023–2026 learning curve showing how ability prediction improves with the number of labeled people.

A supervised model does not escape the harness; it inherits surface bonuses by construction. Root type-token ratio (word types divided by √tokens) rises when jargon adds new word types. A Wolfram-style model would therefore likely reward the jargon-padding probe unless padding is controlled.

Perplexity features are worse, because they move in opposite directions for different groups. L2 writers produce lower LLM surprisal through formulaic vocabulary. That is why seven AI-text detectors unanimously flagged 18 of 91 TOEFL essays, and those essays had significantly lower perplexity ([Liang et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.02819)). Errors raise perplexity instead: error-corrected learner essays score lower than the originals ([Muñoz Sánchez et al. 2024](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2024.bea-1.34)). A perplexity feature would push the harness's non-native and phone-typed probes in opposite directions, neither of them related to reasoning.

The harness's own surface-baseline rater, which only counts word shapes, shows what a presentation-only model looks like: **+18.8 for padding and −2.2 for broken reasoning**. Any supervised scorer the user builds should pass the harness before it sees a real person.

Aggregation over many messages has clean math and a hard ceiling. Attention-based multiple-instance learning pools a person's messages with learned weights a_j = softmax(wᵀtanh(Vh_j)) ([Ilse, Tomczak & Welling 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.04712)), and for personality, attention over whole messages beat attention over words ([Lynn et al. 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.472/)). Both need many labeled people, because labels exist only at the person level. Under a Gaussian random-effects model, the optimal shrinkage of a person's mean toward the population mean is B_i = n_iρ₁/(1 + (n_i−1)ρ₁). That is exactly the Spearman-Brown reliability of the person's mean (Kelley's formula; [empirical Bayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_Bayes_method)). People with few messages should therefore get estimates near 100 with wide ranges. The companion analysis gives the validity ceiling, r₁/√ρ₁, that more chats cannot pass (§8).

Shrinkage implies a probe the harness lacks: give the rater one message, three messages, and all of a persona's messages. A calibrated rater pulls its estimate toward 100 and widens its range as messages are removed. A rater that reports a point estimate from the evidence alone, with no prior, will not. Qwen's offset and its full-SD swings on a single message suggest it behaves this way (companion §4).

Information theory sets the outer bound. For Gaussian IQ, R² ≤ 1 − exp(−2I) for any predictor built from any representation of the text, where I is the mutual information between text and IQ, and no processing adds information ([mutual information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_information)). The companion analysis tabulates what that means: Wolfram's essays carry about 0.6 bits (§5). Two consequences are not in that table. A predictor that minimizes squared error is calibrated on average but regresses individuals toward the mean: a person with true IQ 130 is predicted at about 100 + R²·30, **117 even at Wolfram-level R² = .57**. And stretching predictions back to an SD of 15, as "IQ estimate" products tend to, always loses accuracy: mean squared error becomes 2σ²(1−r) instead of σ²(1−r²), an RMSE of **10.6 instead of 9.9 at r = .75, and 17.6 instead of 14.3 at r = .31** (my calculation).

What the number measures matters as much as its precision. Text predicts verbal, reading and school-achievement-like ability, so a chat-derived number should be labeled a verbal or crystallized indicator unless it has been validated against non-verbal tests. Removing the part of both predictions and IQ that education explains gives a "partial" validity. It is a lower bound, because education is partly caused by ability. Treating first-language groups as separate environments for invariant risk minimization is possible ([Arjovsky et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02893)), but no one has tested it for ability. Where group differences appear, they tend to be in calibration, not rank order. In Peters & Matz, absolute errors differed by gender and age while within-group correlations did not, which calls for recalibrating each group separately.

EQ inherits every limit above, with a noisier criterion. Ability-EI tests are short and unreliable: STEU-B has α ≈ .63–.72 and STEM-B ≈ .75–.84 ([Yan et al. 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01156/full)), and newer tests average item-total correlations of .18 ([Schlegel et al. 2025](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12095572/)). Against STEU-B, even a perfect predictor cannot show r above √.63 ≈ .79. With a chat measure of reliability .70 the ceiling is .66, and a true correlation of .50 appears as .33. Ability EI correlates about .25 with g ([Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1635847/full)). The one study that predicted EI from Reddit text reported an adjusted R² of 9–15.6%, with no holdout set ([Dover & Amichai-Hamburger 2023](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130158/)).

The methods transfer better to rubric coding of observable empathic behavior. There, agreement between LLMs and experts (median weighted κ .60) matched agreement between experts (.58), but fell to κ .29 for items that require inferences about inner states ([Kumar et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10150v2)). G-theory sizes such a coder's reliability, and conformal intervals bound a profile score. The harness's matched-guise design applies unchanged: the same empathic moves written in African American English, non-native English or WeChat-casual Chinese should earn the same codes.

## Adopt in this order: precision, mechanism, refusals, then validation

The ranking weighs how much each method changes the conclusions already drawn against what it costs. The first six need no human subjects and no new infrastructure.

| # | Adopt | What changes in iqaudit | Cost | Why at this rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Persona-level small-sample intervals, 12 personas, flawed × surface cells | Report t-intervals (3 df now, 11 at 12 personas) beside the bootstrap; write 8 more personas; add flawed versions of the non-native, dialect and jargon variants to test non-uniform DIF | Persona authoring, plus roughly 1,000 calls per model for the neutral and ignore-surface framings | The headline covert finding does not yet exclude zero under a t-interval, and every item below needs this precision |
| 2 | Overt-versus-covert difference-in-differences table | Report B, V, ΔB and ΔV per probe from the existing rationales coder, with intervals that resample personas | Near zero | Turns the strongest qualitative finding into a quantity that can be tested |
| 3 | Mechanism tests for the non-native penalty | Double-rewrite canonicalize-then-score (canonicalize base, non-native and flawed versions); extract-then-score pipeline; a "non-native writer" label on base text; a native-speaker detection question | About 100–200 calls | Separates the perceived-clarity path from the identity-stereotype path, and yields a fix if canonicalizing works |
| 4 | Refusal-aware sycophancy reporting and Bayesian claim framings | Two-part summary (decline rate, shift among answered cases, Lee bounds); control effect under every framing; framings with a self-claim, a third-party claim and a proctored-score claim; pushback with and without supporting evidence | Low | The current flattering result is uninterpretable, and refusals depend on content in both models |
| 5 | Rater-facet bookkeeping | Fixed anchor set re-run on every pass; model version as a facet; an ordinal model for the round-number outputs; one crossed mixed model (or Bayesian MFRM) with persona, persona × variant, model × variant and framing × variant terms; report Φ | Analysis only | Version drift and the 5-point Qwen–Gemini severity gap are as large as the biases being audited |
| 6 | Evidence-quantity probe | Logs truncated to 1, 3 and all messages; check that estimates shrink toward 100 and ranges widen; compare stated ranges with honest widths | Low | The only test of calibration that needs no ground truth |
| 7 | Invariance tooling for any trained scorer | Harness variants as augmentation pairs; matched-pair or conditional MMD penalty; LEACE with a linear head; LC-style adjustment only for reporting, with coefficients identified by the harness | Medium | Matters only once a scorer exists, and the harness is its acceptance test |
| 8 | Validation-study design | Logged sampling probabilities, with flagged groups oversampled; stratified PPI++ or DSL for means and group gaps; Mondrian CQR ranges with per-group coverage and width; cluster-robust errors for chat-level data; HRM or empirical-Bayes aggregation; verbal and non-verbal criteria; observed, disattenuated and education-partial validity | High: hundreds to about 1,000 tested people, plus ethics approval | The only route to accuracy claims, and its design must be fixed before data collection |
| 9 | Activation-level probes on an open-weight Qwen | Non-native and jargon directions from matched pairs; steering against random-direction controls; projection as a risk flag | Research project | Mechanistic confirmation of the covert pattern; optional |

Several popular moves are wrong for this project. SIMEX and other classical measurement-error corrections assume the error is the same in every group, which the harness has refuted. Counting repeat calls as information overstates precision, since the HRM and G-theory agree that personas and chats carry information, not calls. Perplexity and surprisal should stay out of any ability score unless the text is error-corrected and compared against a topic-matched baseline. What a model says about its reasons is never evidence of invariance. Rescaling predictions to an SD of 15 always loses accuracy. And equal mean scores across language groups is the wrong fairness target; the right one is the conditional f ⊥ S | Y.

## Conclusion

The center of gravity in this literature has moved from "can a model predict the trait?" to "what can be said validly with a biased predictor and a handful of ground-truth labels?" That shift changes what the harness is. It is not a preliminary to the science; it is the bias model the science needs. Its controlled shifts define the strata for stratified PPI and the groups for Mondrian conformal. Its broken-reasoning control anchors what a construct-relevant change looks like. And its probes are the acceptance test for any scorer, zero-shot or supervised. The same literature shows that the harness's binding constraint is its small number of personas, not the number of calls. Its strongest finding, a covert non-native penalty that survives an explicit instruction, is for now a well-motivated hypothesis carried by three personas.

The most transferable lesson is about how debiasing claims get made. Every mitigation the harness tested or the literature offers (instructions, regression adjustment, erasure, invariance penalties) improves something measurable while leaving something else untouched. Qwen's instruction sharpened the reasoning signal and removed every mention of grammar, which improves every ratio and every explanation. The absolute penalty, the quantity that drives adverse impact, did not move. An audit catches this when it reports absolute shifts with small-sample intervals, refusal rates beside means, and behavior separately from explanations. An audit that reports only ratios and rationales does not.
