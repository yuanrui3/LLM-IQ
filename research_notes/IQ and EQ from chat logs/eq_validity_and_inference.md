# Emotional Intelligence (EQ/EI): Construct Validity, Measurability, and Inference from Text/Chat

Scope note: EQ only. IQ-from-text, general LLM chat-log inference, and ethics/legal/commercial tools are covered by other researchers. Research date 2026-11-13. "EI" = academic term (emotional intelligence); "EQ" = popular label; "情商" = Chinese popular term.

## Q1. Is EI a scientifically valid, measurable construct? (ability vs. trait vs. mixed models; test reliability and factor structure)

### Takeaway
"EI" is not one construct but three families of measures that barely overlap in meaning. Ability EI (MSCEIT, STEU/STEM, GECo) is a modest but real cognitive ability, a narrow facet of intelligence, with psychometric weak spots. Trait/self-report EI (TEIQue, WLEIS) and mixed models (Goleman, Bar-On EQ-i) are mostly personality under another name. The strong popular claims, "EQ matters more than IQ" and Goleman-style leadership claims, are not supported.

### Cited Findings
**The three streams and their critics**
- Joseph & Newman (2010, *Journal of Applied Psychology* 95:54–78) proposed a "cascading model" for ability EI: emotion perception comes before emotion understanding, which comes before emotion regulation and then job performance. They called mixed EI a "grab bag of everything that is not cognitive ability." — [Joseph & Newman, Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/); [Illinois Experts record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/emotional-intelligence-an-integrative-meta-analysis-and-cascading/)
- Joseph, Jin, Newman & O'Boyle (2015, JAP) argued that mixed-EI measures were built by "heterogeneous domain sampling": items pulled from established domains such as personality, self-efficacy and self-rated performance and pooled under the EI label. That explains why mixed EI appears to predict outcomes. — [Joseph & Newman, Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)
- Most mixed-EI items can be classified as Big Five personality items (de Raad, 2005, as summarized by Joseph & Newman). — [Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)
- Locke (2005) argued that "the whole conceptualization of EI is fatally flawed." He and Landy (2005) are described as the most vehement I-O psychology critics, who argued that self-report EI is confounded with other constructs. — quoted in [Antonakis et al. 2009 record, serval.unil.ch](https://serval.unil.ch/fr/notice/serval:BIB_F989E68B6F71); [Emotion Researcher "Reintroducing EI"](https://emotionresearcher.com/reintroducing-emotional-intelligence-what-it-is-and-where-we-stand-now/)
- Antonakis, Ashkanasy & Dasborough (2009, *Leadership Quarterly* 20:247–261), "Does leadership need emotional intelligence?": Antonakis argued that claims of EI predicting leadership can be explained by established personality and intelligence theories. Ashkanasy and Dasborough accepted many of the concerns but placed the problems mainly in self-report measures, not ability measures. The debate was revisited in Dasborough et al. (2022, LQ), "Does leadership still not need emotional intelligence? Continuing 'The Great EI Debate'." — [serval.unil.ch](https://serval.unil.ch/fr/notice/serval:BIB_F989E68B6F71); [Lancaster eprints](https://eprints.lancs.ac.uk/id/eprint/155893)
- Ability EI behaves like a facet of intelligence. MacCann et al. (2014, *Emotion*) is cited for ability EI fitting as a second-stratum factor in intelligence models. — [Joseph & Newman, Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)

**MSCEIT (the flagship ability test): reliability and structure problems**
- Consensus scoring has been criticized as sensitive to irrelevant response characteristics such as score elevation and scatter (Legree et al., 2014). Defenders reply that consensus and expert scoring correlate .93–.99. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025, MSCEIT 2 paper](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1539785/full)
- Factor structure findings are mixed. Rossen et al. (2008) found the theory-based hierarchical model fit improperly. Keele & Bell (2008) supported three factors. Another study supported four first-order factors but no higher-order global EI factor. — [Frontiers 2025 (MSCEIT 2)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1539785/full); [Fiori et al. 2014, PLoS ONE](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/173475/1/Fiori%20Antonietti%20Mikolajczak%20et%20al%202014%20PlosOne.pdf)
- Fiori & Antonakis (2011): MSCEIT scores are largely predicted by personality, general intelligence and demographics. Maul (2012): the MSCEIT may measure construct-irrelevant abilities, such as matching emotions to colors and tastes. — as cited in [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1539785/full) and the [Fiori et al. 2014 PDF](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/173475/1/Fiori%20Antonietti%20Mikolajczak%20et%20al%202014%20PlosOne.pdf)
- Fiori et al. (2014, IRT analysis): the MSCEIT discriminates best among people at the low end of the trait, so it is poor at separating average from high EI. — [Fiori et al. 2014](https://orbi.uliege.be/bitstream/2268/173475/1/Fiori%20Antonietti%20Mikolajczak%20et%20al%202014%20PlosOne.pdf)
- Palmer et al. (2005) found lower reliability for some MSCEIT subscales. The developers' 2025 revision (MSCEIT 2) drops consensus scoring for veridical/expert scoring and reports acceptable reliability for 3 of 4 subscale scores. — [Frontiers 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1539785/full)

**Newer ability tests (STEU, STEM, GEMOK, GECo)**
- STEU (Situational Test of Emotion Understanding): 42 short vignettes. Typical item: "A supervisor who is unpleasant to work for leaves Alfonso's work. Alfonso is most likely to feel?" with five emotion words as options. — [Schlegel, Sommer & Mortillaro 2025, Communications Psychology (UNIGE copy)](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:185363)
- GEMOK-Blends: 20 vignettes of 100–140 words, scored against appraisal-theory cue patterns, validated in 180 MTurk workers. GECo (Geneva Emotional Competence test; Schlegel & Mortillaro, JAP 2018/2019) has four subtests: emotion recognition from nonverbal cues, understanding, self-regulation, and management of others. The regulation subtest has 28 workplace vignettes scored by adaptive vs. maladaptive strategies (reappraisal, acceptance vs. rumination, catastrophizing). — [Schlegel et al. 2025](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:185363); [GECo, UNIGE](https://access.archive-ouverte.unige.ch/access/metadata/28c0bbae-60f1-48aa-8375-96567ad41a81/download)
- Internal consistency of these tests is low. In Schlegel et al. (2025), the average item-total correlation of the original STEU-B, STEM-B, GEMOK-Blends, GECo-Regulation and GECo-Management tests was r = 0.183. — [Schlegel et al. 2025, full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12095572/)
- Simonet et al. (2021, *Journal of Intelligence*), on GECo: only the emotion-regulation subtest has enough reliable variance beyond a general-ability factor to be used as a stand-alone score. Recognition and regulation predicted GPA and affective engagement incrementally over fluid ability and the Big Five, but in opposite directions. — [Simonet et al. 2021, PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8006218)

**Popular "EQ" claims**
- MacCann et al. (2020) ranked EI the third most important predictor of academic performance, behind intelligence and conscientiousness. That directly contradicts "EQ matters more than IQ" for academic outcomes. — [MacCann et al. 2020, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31829667/); [Oxford Education news](https://www.education.ox.ac.uk/meta-analysis-shows-that-emotional-intelligence-predicts-academic-performance/)
- 36Kr (2026-07-31) carried a translated popular critique, "我不再相信'情商'这套理论了". It argues that EQ rides on an analogy with IQ but lacks a unified, quantifiable standard, that Goleman's 1990s popularization targeted business audiences, and that the theory "mixed in a small amount of real psychology." — [36Kr](https://www.36kr.com/p/3879918269329417)

### Inferences
- "Is EQ a thing?" A defensible answer: a narrow, measurable ability to reason about emotions exists (ability EI). It correlates moderately with g and adds a little prediction in emotion-heavy contexts. The broad popular "EQ" is mostly agreeableness, emotional stability, extraversion and conscientiousness plus self-confidence, renamed. "EQ beats IQ" is not supported.
- For a measurement project, "EQ" must be operationalized as one specific construct. The three families have different ground truths (maximal-performance test, self-report questionnaire, or observer ratings), and they do not converge.
- Ability EI tests have modest reliability (item-total about .18; MSCEIT discriminates mainly at the low end). Any validation target is noisy, which caps the correlation any predictor, including an LLM reading chats, can reach.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the original MSCEIT manual reliability figures (full-scale split-half) or GECo's original reliability and validity coefficients. The secondary sources gave none.
- I did not retrieve the agreeableness-specific meta-analytic ρ with ability EI from Joseph & Newman (2010).

## Q2. Is EI largely redundant with the Big Five plus g? Incremental validity, quantified

### Takeaway
Trait/self-report EI is close to redundant with personality: it correlates about .85 with the general factor of personality, with a genetic correlation of about .90. Ability EI is more distinct (about .28 with GFP, about .25 with g), but its incremental validity over Big Five plus GMA for job performance is close to zero (ΔR² = .002). Mixed EI's apparently large incremental validity (ΔR² = .07–.14) reflects item content overlap with self-rated performance and self-efficacy, not a new construct. The best case for EI is academic performance (ρ = .20; ability EI ρ = .24), where it adds a little beyond IQ and Big Five.

### Cited Findings
**Overlap with personality (GFP) and g**
- van der Linden et al. (2017, *Psychological Bulletin* 143:36–52), meta-analysis of 142 sources, N = 36,268: GFP with trait EI r ≈ .85; GFP with ability EI r ≈ .28. The authors suggest the GFP and trait EI may be "very similar, perhaps even synonymous." — [Tilburg repository](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/overlap-between-the-general-factor-of-personality-and-emotional-i/); [gwern PDF](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/personality/2017-vanderlinden.pdf)
- van der Linden et al. (2018, *Behavior Genetics*), twin study: genetic correlation between GFP and trait EI r = .90; heritability 53% (GFP) and 45% (trait EI). — [VU Amsterdam](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/overlap-between-the-general-factor-of-personality-and-trait-emoti/); [EUR repository](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/103608)
- Ability EI with cognitive ability: meta-analytic ρ = 0.25 (Joseph & Newman, 2010, as cited). Viswesvaran & Pluta's meta-analytic review reports ability-based EI correlating .34 with cognitive ability vs. .13 for mixed-model EI, with mixed measures overlapping more with personality. — [Frontiers in Psychology 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2025.1635847/full)

**Incremental validity for job performance (over Big Five + GMA)**
- Ability EI: ΔR² = .002, i.e. 0.2% (Joseph & Newman, 2010); ΔR² = .004, i.e. 0.4% (O'Boyle et al., 2011). In high-emotional-labor jobs, ability EI ΔR² = .015, i.e. 1.5% (Joseph & Newman, 2010; small subgroup samples). — [Joseph & Newman, Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)
- Mixed EI: ΔR² = .142, i.e. 14% (Joseph & Newman, 2010); ΔR² = .068, i.e. 7% (O'Boyle et al., 2011). Joseph et al. (2015) attribute this to heterogeneous domain sampling: the items include self-efficacy, self-rated performance and similar content. — [Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)
- Sex differences (Joseph & Newman, 2010): MSCEIT ability EI d = .52 favoring women; self-report ability EI d = .01; self-report mixed EI d = .02. — [Emotion Researcher 2015](https://emotionresearcher.com/emotional-intelligence-some-research-findings-and-remaining-questions/)

**Academic performance (the best evidence for EI)**
- MacCann et al. (2020, *Psychological Bulletin* 146(2):150–186): overall ρ = .20 (N = 42,529; k = 1,246 effects from 158 sources). Ability EI ρ = .24 (k = 50), self-rated EI ρ = .12 (k = 33), mixed EI ρ = .19 (k = 90). EI ranked third after intelligence and conscientiousness. Ability EI predicted more strongly in the humanities than in the sciences. Causal direction is not established. — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31829667/); [Oxford Education](https://www.education.ox.ac.uk/meta-analysis-shows-that-emotional-intelligence-predicts-academic-performance/); [Sydney Uni news](https://sydney.edu.au/news-opinion/news/2020/02/03/students-with-high-emotional-intelligence-get-better-grades.html)

**Trait EI (TEIQue) incremental validity**
- Andrei, Siegling, Aloe, Baldaro & Petrides (2016, *Journal of Personality Assessment* 98(3):261–276) reviewed 24 articles with 114 incremental-validity analyses and meta-analyzed 18 studies (105 effect sizes). They concluded the TEIQue "consistently explains incremental variance" for outcomes such as depression, anxiety, coping and life satisfaction. A later review notes that only 5 of 24 studies reported significant incremental contributions at the facet/factor level; the rest relied on the global score. — [Univ. of Iowa record](https://iro.uiowa.edu/esploro/outputs/journalArticle/The-Incremental-Validity-of-the-Trait/9984371267802771); [Frontiers 2018 review (UCL)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10067732/1/fpsyg-09-02786.pdf)

### Inferences
- Rough quantitative picture. Trait EI is about 72% shared variance with GFP (r ≈ .85 squared), so an "EQ score" inferred from chats with self-report EI as ground truth would be mostly a personality score: high agreeableness, emotional stability and extraversion. Ability EI shares only about 6% of variance with g (ρ = .25 squared) and about 8% with GFP (.28 squared), so it is more distinct, but it adds almost nothing to job-performance prediction once g and Big Five are known.
- If an LLM infers both IQ and EQ from chats, the obvious confound is that each estimate will partly reflect the other plus personality and writing fluency. The project should test whether the EQ estimate predicts a validated EI test over and above LLM-inferred Big Five and IQ, the same incremental-validity test the field uses.
- The incremental-validity numbers I found are for job and academic performance. For well-being and mental-health outcomes, trait EI shows more incremental variance (Andrei et al. 2016), partly because those outcomes are also self-reported (shared method variance).

### Gaps
- I did not access Andrei et al. (2016) full text, so the average ΔR² for the TEIQue is not reported here.
- I found no single meta-analysis giving facet-level Big Five ρ values (e.g., agreeableness and neuroticism) for ability EI and trait EI side by side. Joseph & Newman (2010) Table 3 likely has them but was not retrieved.
- MacCann et al. (2020) incremental R² values over intelligence and Big Five were not retrieved; only the relative-importance ranking was.

## Q3. Has anyone predicted EI scores from text, social media, essays or conversations, and how accurately?

### Takeaway
Very little exists, and none of it is validated against an ability-EI test. The main study (Dover & Amichai-Hamburger, 2023, *Scientific Reports*) predicted a short self-report EI scale (WLEIS) from Reddit posts using LIWC. It reached in-sample adjusted R² of 9–15.6% per dimension (multiple R ≈ .30–.40), with no out-of-sample validation and internal inconsistencies in the paper. Other "EI from Twitter" papers use sentiment counts as proxies, with no criterion test. I found no study using an LLM to infer a person's EI score from their text and validating it against an EI test.

### Cited Findings
- **Dover & Amichai-Hamburger (2023), "Characteristics of online user-generated text predict the emotional intelligence of individuals," *Scientific Reports*:**
  - Data: 681 Reddit volunteers, 606 with both the WLEIS (16-item self-report; dimensions SEA, OEA, UOE, ROE) and public text. Up to 2,000 recent posts per user; mean 30,258 words, median 8,375.
  - Method: 104 LIWC-22 variables; correlations plus OLS stepwise regression.
  - Results: single-variable correlations ranged about −0.20 to 0.18 (e.g., positive emotion with Others'-Emotion-Appraisal r = .18; negative tone with Use-of-Emotion r = −.20). Adjusted R² was 9% (SEA), 15.4% (OEA), 13.2% (UOE) and 15.6% (ROE), all in-sample.
  - Problems: no train/test split; the text reports R² of "6 to 10%" while the table shows 9–15.6%; implied correlations are stated inconsistently (0.24–0.32 vs. 0.30–0.40).
  - Stated limitations: self-report criterion, self-selected sample, LIWC less flexible than LLMs, no demographic controls.
  - Pattern: higher EI went with more positive-affect, less negative-affect, more social and more "Clout" language.
  - Source: [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10130158/); [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-33907-4)
- "Sensing Users' Emotional Intelligence in Social Networks" (e3s conference, 2023, and an earlier ResearchGate version) estimates EI on Twitter from sentiment counts and follower/retweet features, without a validated EI criterion. A Medium post on the same idea concedes its approach needs real text data and better NLP. These are not credible evidence of accuracy. — [e3s PDF](https://www.e3s-conferences.org/articles/e3sconf/pdf/2023/67/e3sconf_icmpc2023_01078.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/337988466_Sensing_Users'_Emotional_Intelligence_in_Social_Networks); [Medium](https://medium.com/@apshana/predicting-users-emotional-intelligence-based-on-social-networking-data-9a988ea81e15)
- **Adjacent: empathy as a trait, predicted from social media language**
  - Abdul-Mageed et al. (2017, ICWSM) predicted "pathogenic empathy" from Facebook posts against a new questionnaire: Pearson r = 0.252. — [ICWSM](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/ICWSM/article/view/14942)
  - Litvak et al. (2016, PEOPLES @ COLING) predicted Interpersonal Reactivity Index empathy subscales from Facebook text and friending behavior. — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/W16-4314)
  - Yaden et al. (2023, *Emotion*) analyzed 2,356,916 Facebook posts from N = 2,781 people. With shared variance controlled, high-empathy people used more self-focused, negative, isolated and "overwhelmed" language; high-compassion people used other-focused, positive language. — [Stony Brook](https://researchconnect.stonybrook.edu/en/publications/characterizing-empathy-and-compassion-using-computational-linguis/); [PDF](https://sjgiorgi.github.io/publications/yadengiorgi2023characterizing.pdf)
- **Adjacent benchmark ceiling (personality, since trait EI ≈ personality):**
  - LLMs inferring Big Five zero-shot from social media posts: average r = .29 with self-report (Peters et al., *PNAS Nexus* 2024). — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11211928/)
  - LLMs inferring BFI-10 from 555 real interviews: correlations from −0.18 to 0.27. — [arXiv 2507.14355](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.14355)

### Inferences
- The realistic ceiling for predicting self-report trait EI from a person's text is probably about r = .2–.4, similar to text-based personality inference. That follows from trait EI ≈ GFP and from Dover & Amichai-Hamburger's in-sample R of about .3–.4, which will shrink out of sample.
- Predicting ability EI from chats is a harder, essentially untested problem. Ability tests measure maximal performance on emotion-reasoning puzzles, while chats show typical behavior. Schlegel et al. (2025) note that "maximal performance in emotion-related tasks often differs from typical performance." A person can know the right answer and not act on it in their own chats, and vice versa.
- Because ability tests are short, cheap and LLM-generatable (STEU/STEM are free for research; GECo is available for research), a project wanting ground truth should administer one directly rather than rely on self-report.

### Gaps
- I found no peer-reviewed study (through Nov 2026) in which an LLM rates an individual's EI from their own chat or text and is validated against the MSCEIT, STEU/STEM, GECo or TEIQue. This appears to be an open area.
- I found no study predicting EI from private messaging (WeChat, WhatsApp) logs, as opposed to public posts.
- WASSA shared tasks on predicting self-reported empathy and distress from essays exist, but I did not retrieve their accuracy figures.

## Q4. LLMs and EI: LLMs taking EI tests, LLM "EQ" benchmarks, and LLMs judging human empathy in dialogue

### Takeaway
LLMs ace text-based ability-EI tests: 81% vs. a 56% human average across five tests (Schlegel, Sommer & Mortillaro, 2025). That shows these tests measure emotion knowledge that can be solved from text, and it makes LLMs plausible judges of emotional reasoning. LLM "EQ" benchmarks (EQ-Bench, EmoBench, newer conversation-based ones) measure the model, not people; EQ-Bench correlates r = 0.97 with MMLU, i.e. largely general capability. The most validated adjacent area is LLM-as-judge for empathic communication in text conversations. Kumar et al. (2025/2026, *Nature Machine Intelligence*) show LLM-expert agreement (median weighted κ 0.60) on par with expert-expert agreement (median 0.58) and far above crowdworkers (0.33). These are message-level ratings of observable empathic behaviors, not person-level EI scores.

### Cited Findings
**Schlegel, Sommer & Mortillaro (2025), *Communications Psychology* 3:80, doi:10.1038/s44271-025-00258-x**
- Tests: STEM (emotion management), STEU (emotion understanding), GEMOK-Blends, GECo Emotion Regulation, GECo Emotion Management.
- Models: ChatGPT-4, ChatGPT-o1, Gemini 1.5 Flash, Copilot 365, Claude 3.5 Haiku, DeepSeek V3, each run 10 times per test in Dec 2024–Jan 2025.
- Mean accuracy across LLMs was 81%, vs. 56% for the human validation samples. Every LLM scored more than 1 SD above the human mean; ChatGPT-o1 and DeepSeek V3 exceeded 2 SD. LLMs beat humans on each of the five tests individually, with large effect sizes.
- Inter-LLM agreement across 105 items: ICC = .88. Item-level correlation between human and LLM proportion correct: r = 0.46.
- ChatGPT-4-generated test versions given to N = 467 Prolific participants (US/UK) matched the originals' difficulty. Clarity, diversity, internal consistency and validity were not statistically equivalent, but all differences were |d| < 0.25. Original and generated versions correlated r = 0.46.
- Authors' limitations: standardized multiple-choice tests "may not fully capture the complexities of real-world emotional interactions"; LLMs are prompt-sensitive; Western-centric tests and training data, with display rules and regulation norms that vary across cultures; black-box behavior. They also note that "maximal performance in emotion-related tasks often differs from typical performance."
- Sources: [UNIGE record](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:185363); [PMC full text](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12095572/); [UNIGE press release](https://www.unige.ch/medias/en/2025/et-si-lia-comprenait-les-emotions-mieux-que-nous)

**LLM "EQ" benchmarks (these evaluate the model)**
- EQ-Bench (Paech, arXiv 2312.06281, Dec 2023): 60 English questions in which the model rates the intensity of characters' emotions in GPT-4-generated conflict dialogues. It correlates r = 0.97 with MMLU and r = 0.91 with HellaSwag. Reference answers were set by the authors; no human baseline in v1. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06281v2)
- EmoBench (Sabour et al., ACL 2024): 400 hand-crafted questions in English and Chinese covering Emotional Understanding and Emotional Application. The best model then (GPT-4) fell short of the average human, with perspective-taking weakest. — [arXiv 2402.12071](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.12071v3); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2402.12071)
- Newer 2026 conversation-based LLM-EI benchmarks: AttuneBench (arXiv 2605.21739) and SpeechEQ (arXiv 2606.25990). SpeechEQ explicitly states its score is "not a validated psychological assessment." HEART (Hippocratic AI, Jan 2026) reports frontier models at about 78.7% agreement with human evaluators on emotional-support dialogue. — [AttuneBench](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.21739v2); [SpeechEQ](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.25990); [HEART PDF](https://hippocraticai.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/HEART_-A-Unified-Benchmark-for-Assessing-Humans-and-LLMs-in-Emotional-Support-Dialogue.pdf)

**Empathy and supportiveness scoring from text (the validated adjacent area)**
- EPITOME (Sharma, Miner, Atkins & Althoff, EMNLP 2020) adapts face-to-face empathy scales to text with three mechanisms: Emotional Reactions, Interpretations and Explorations, each rated none/weak/strong. Corpus: 10k annotated (post, response) pairs from Reddit/TalkLife with rationales. A RoBERTa multi-task model reached about 80% accuracy and about 70% macro-F1 (accuracy figures are from a secondary summary). Applied to 235k TalkLife conversations, it found that peer supporters do not become more empathic over time. — [arXiv 2009.08441](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2009.08441); [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2020.emnlp-main.425)
- **Kumar, Poungpeth, Yang, Farrell, Lambert & Groh, "When large language models are reliable for judging empathic communication"** (arXiv 2506.10150, June 2025; *Nature Machine Intelligence* 2025/2026, doi:10.1038/s42256-025-01169-6):
  - Data: 200 real text conversations, 50 from each of four frameworks: Empathetic Dialogues, EPITOME, Perceived Empathy (Yin et al. 2024) and the authors' new "Lend an Ear."
  - Annotations: 3,150 expert, 2,844 crowd, 3,150 LLM (primarily Gemini 2.5 Pro; GPT-4o and Claude 3.7 Sonnet in the appendix).
  - Agreement: expert-expert Krippendorff's α 0.29–0.78 (median 0.55) and weighted κ median 0.58. LLM-expert weighted κ 0.17–0.86, median 0.60, above the high-agreement threshold on 15 of 21 sub-components. Crowd-expert median κ 0.33. Crowds showed positive rating inflation, and neither training nor aggregation closed the gap.
  - Caveats: sub-components requiring inferred intentions or inner states had the lowest agreement (EPITOME Interpretations median κ 0.29). Results are prompt-specific and stranger-only, with no longitudinal or intimate relationships.
  - Sources: [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10150v2); [Nature MI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-025-01169-6); [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2025/when-large-language-models-are-reliable-for-judging-empathic/)
- A 2026 Japanese empathetic medical dialogue dataset (EmplifAI) found LLM-judge scores correlated strongly or moderately with human evaluators for most models. — [arXiv 2601.10033](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10033)
- Text-based emotion inference and empathetic response by LLMs vs. human counselors has been evaluated (2025, *Computers in Human Behavior Reports*). — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2451958825003197)

### Inferences
- LLMs are good judges of emotion reasoning in text, so the bottleneck for an "EQ-from-chats" system is not the LLM's own emotional understanding. It is construct definition and ground truth: what person-level quantity is being estimated, and what it is validated against.
- The most defensible design for a chat-based "EQ" estimate is behavioral coding. Use an LLM to rate observable conversational behaviors message by message with explicit rubrics (EPITOME's reactions, interpretations and explorations; Lend an Ear's validating emotions vs. unsolicited advice; de-escalation; asking about others' feelings), then aggregate across many conversations. Kumar et al. show this is reliable when rubrics are specific. Whether such aggregates correlate with ability or trait EI tests is unknown and would need to be checked.
- Kumar et al.'s finding that inner-state and intention judgments are least reliable warns against having an LLM directly output a global "EQ = 115" number from a chat log. Global holistic ratings are the least reliable kind.
- LLMs' near-ceiling performance on ability-EI tests also implies those tests are partly verbal-reasoning puzzles. Ability EI in humans correlates with g and vocabulary, so an LLM's estimate of a person's ability EI from text may mostly track verbal ability, overlapping with the IQ estimate.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve EmoBench's exact human vs. model accuracy, or EQ-Bench 3's (2025) methodology.
- I found no published validation of LLM person-level EI ratings from chats against MSCEIT, GECo, STEU or TEIQue.
- I found no study on whether message-level empathy scores (EPITOME etc.) aggregated per person correlate with that person's ability or trait EI.

## Q5. Chinese popular 情商 vs. academic EI; Chinese tools that rate 情商 from chats

### Takeaway
In everyday Chinese usage, 情商 usually means social savvy: tact, saying the right thing (会说话), face-work, reading the room and managing superiors (人情世故). That is closer to culturally specific social-interaction skill, and sometimes to conformity or ingratiation, than to the academic construct of perceiving, understanding and regulating emotions (情绪智力). Chinese writers themselves call the "情商 = 会说话" equation a misreading. The Chinese "情商" apps I found are reply generators or relationship-chat analyzers, not validated assessments, and I found no Chinese tool that produces a psychometrically validated 情商 score from chats.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- If the user's audience means 情商 in the popular sense, the target is closer to observable, culture-specific social-communication skill (tact, face-saving, appropriate deference, de-escalation, warmth) than to Western ability EI. It may also partly capture strategic ingratiation, which can be valued differently by different raters. No validated instrument for this popular 情商 turned up. A chat-based rubric would need its own norms, ideally from Chinese raters, and could not borrow MSCEIT- or GECo-style validity.
- Chats are a natural place to observe popular 情商 (it is defined by what you say to whom). But "high-EQ reply" norms depend on relationship (boss, partner, friend), so any scoring must be relationship-conditional, and a single number across contexts will be noisy.
- Existing Chinese apps help users produce "high-EQ" replies; they do not assess the user. That leaves an open space but also no validated precedent to benchmark against.

### Gaps
- I found no CNKI or Chinese-journal (e.g., 心理学报, 心理科学) empirical study comparing a Chinese indigenous 情商 structure with WLEIS, MSCEIT or TEIQue factor structures; the search returned no such paper. The WLEIS (Wong & Law, developed in Hong Kong) is the most-used EI scale in Chinese samples, but I did not retrieve sources on its Chinese validation.
- I found no Chinese app or tool that outputs a numeric 情商 score from chat logs with any validation evidence.
- Zhihu discussions were not directly retrieved; the popular-usage evidence comes from course pages, opinion essays and Sixth Tone journalism.
