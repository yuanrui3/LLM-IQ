# Ethics, legal constraints, commercial landscape and validation methodology for inferring IQ / EQ / personality from chat text

Research date: 2026-11-13. Informational only, not legal advice. Notes on sourcing: items marked "(regulation text, not re-fetched this session)" cite the official text from memory of its wording; the report-writer should treat them as reliable but may want to spot-check. Items marked "(search snippet; page not opened)" come only from search-result summaries.

## Q1. Commercial / industry landscape: who has inferred personality or cognitive ability from language, what validation they published, and how they fared

### Takeaway
No mainstream vendor I found claims to estimate IQ from free-form text; the language-based products infer personality or "competencies" (and the ones that measure cognitive ability use games or conventional test items, not chat). The track record is poor: IBM sunset Watson Personality Insights (2020-2021), HireVue dropped facial analysis under FTC-complaint pressure (Jan 2021) and was hit by a BIPA class action, independent audits found Humantic AI and Crystal too unstable to count as valid tests, and a 2026 study of 4M+ Pymetrics-screened applications found adverse impact against Black applicants when outcomes were analyzed per position.

### Cited Findings

**HireVue (video/language interview scoring; game-based cognitive tests)**
- In 2019 EPIC filed an FTC complaint against HireVue, alleging its denial that it used facial recognition was a deceptive trade practice; on 12 Jan 2021 HireVue announced it would stop relying on "facial analysis" to assess candidates, about a year after the complaint — [EPIC](https://epic.org/hirevue-facing-ftc-complaint-from-epic-halts-use-of-facial-recognition/); [Wired](https://www.wired.com/story/job-screening-service-halts-facial-analysis-applicants/)
- HireVue's stated rationale was that natural language processing had improved and "visual analysis no longer significantly added value to the assessments" — [SHRM, Feb 2021](https://www.shrm.org/resourcesandtools/hr-topics/talent-acquisition/pages/hirevue-discontinues-facial-analysis-screening.aspx). The change was announced alongside a third-party algorithm audit — [Fortune, 19 Jan 2021](https://fortune.com/2021/01/19/hirevue-drops-facial-monitoring-amid-a-i-algorithm-audit)
- HireVue continued to process biometric data (voice/video), and a class action under Illinois' Biometric Information Privacy Act was filed against it in January 2022 — [Paul Hastings client alert](https://paulhastings.com/insights/client-alerts/class-action-targeting-video-interview-technology-reminds-employers-of)
- HireVue's 2025 AI Explainability Statement says its AI-scored assessments include video interviews, simulations and game-based challenges, and that ML models were trained to relate candidates' behaviour in games to "various cognitive abilities and personality profiles"; scoring models are "designed to mitigate or reduce differences between demographic groups" — [HireVue 2025 AI Explainability Statement (PDF)](https://www.hirevue.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/HV_2025_AI-Explainability-Statement.pdf)
- HireVue reports its interview-transcript language model reaches an average r = 0.69 with expert human rater scores across 99,411 evaluated interview responses (note: criterion is human ratings of answers, not a cognitive test or job performance) — [HireVue AI readiness guide](https://learn.hirevue.com/ai-readiness-guide)
- The Center for Democracy & Technology said the explainability statement "provides virtually no explanation" of how the game-based assessments were devised or tested, that it is unclear how games were validated or tested for accessibility/bias, and noted an inconsistency between claims of measuring "cognitive" and "non-cognitive" abilities vs. "cognitive ability" only — [CDT](https://cdt.org/insights/hirevue-ai-explainability-statement-mostly-fails-to-explain-what-it-does/)

**IBM Watson Personality Insights (Big Five / needs / values from text)**
- Discontinued: no new instances from 1 Dec 2020; existing instances supported until 1 Dec 2021; "no direct replacement" — [IBM Watson SDK docs](https://watson-developer-cloud.github.io/node-sdk/master/classes/personalityinsightsv3.html)
- Reason given publicly by an IBM staffer: PI "has been a frozen service for several years" and would not be merged into Watson NLU — [IBM Community forum](https://community.ibm.com/community/user/ai-datascience/discussion/watson-pi-deprecated). I found no IBM statement attributing the sunset to validity or ethics concerns.

**Humantic AI and Crystal Knows (personality from LinkedIn profiles, resumes, emails)**
- Rhea, Markey, D'Arinzo, Schellmann, Sloane, Squires, Arif Kahn & Stoyanovich audited Humantic AI and Crystal using a stability-audit framework (premise: reliability is necessary but not sufficient for validity) and concluded both show "substantial instability with respect to key facets of measurement" and "cannot be considered valid testing instruments" — [arXiv 2201.09151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09151)
- Reported detail from that audit: Crystal frequently computed different personality scores when the same resume was supplied as PDF vs. raw text; Humantic profiles varied significantly by data source — [AITopics summary of the paper](https://aitopics.org/doc/arxivorg:D0713AE2); [NSF PAR copy](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10352861) (search snippet; page not opened)
- Crystal's own materials claim roughly 85% accuracy against self/peer-reported assessments on one page and 80% on another; no independent validation found — [Crystal accuracy page](https://hubspot.crystalknows.com/resource/crystal-accuracy); [Crystal methodology](https://crystalknows.com/how-predictions-work) (search snippet; pages not opened)
- Gizmodo characterized the category as "AI phrenology" and "extremely unreliable" — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/were-doing-ai-phrenology-again-2000553600)

**Sapia.ai (formerly PredictiveHire) — text chat interviews with personality inference**
- Peer-reviewed paper by Sapia researchers (Dai, Jayaratne, Jayatilleke, Frontiers in Psychology 2022): >58,000 applicants completed a text chat interview plus a HEXACO questionnaire; language-inferred vs. self-rated traits correlated on average r = 0.37 across the six HEXACO dimensions (highest Openness r = 0.45) — [Frontiers in Psychology 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.865841/pdf)
- Caveat: the criterion is self-report questionnaire, not job performance or independent ratings; vendor claims such as "over 80% of candidates hired came from those recommended" are company figures without independent confirmation — [Sapia blog](https://sapia.ai/resources/blog/chat-interviews-predict-job-fit/)

**Pymetrics (games, now owned by Harver) — useful as an audit precedent**
- Wilson et al. (co-authors include Ghosh, Mislove and Pymetrics' Polli), "Building and Auditing Fair Algorithms: A Case Study in Candidate Screening" (FAccT 2021): auditors had code and data access but were limited to the question of model fairness; the engagement was paid sponsored research — [FAccT paper PDF](https://www.ccs.neu.edu/home/amislove/publications/Pymetrics-FAccT.pdf); [MIT Technology Review, Feb 2021](https://www.technologyreview.com/2021/02/11/1017955/auditors-testing-ai-hiring-algorithms-bias-big-questions-remain/)
- Harver/Pymetrics publishes NYC LL144 bias-audit summaries (audits by BABL AI) — [Harver Pymetrics audit summary, June 2023](https://www.paramount.com/sites/g/files/dxjhpe226/files/2023-07/Harver_Pymetrics-Final_Audit_Summary-2023-06-29.pdf)
- 2026 "Algorithmic Monocultures in Hiring" (Stanford, Chapman, Northeastern; FAccT 2026): >4 million applications from 3 million applicants across 156 employers, all screened by Pymetrics; analyzing each of 1,746 positions separately (rather than pooling, as Pymetrics did), 10.62% of positions showed adverse impact on Black applicants; 25.87% of Black applicants' applications (nearly 40,000) went to such positions; 14.74% of Asian applicants' applications went to positions with discriminatory outcomes; among applicants applying to 10 positions, 4% were rejected from all of them, above chance. Harver did not respond to comment — [Fortune, 26 May 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/ai-hiring-algorithm-racial-disparities-pymetrics-stanford-study/)

**Chinese HR tech (example: Beisen 北森)**
- Beisen's AI interview product is at version 2.0, covers 300+ job types, and evaluates candidates across 8 dimensions including general ability, job competency and "psychological risk"; AI interviews typically last 20–30 minutes and include conversation, personality tests and aptitude (行测) items — [界面新闻 Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/13673088.html)
- Beisen markets "human–machine consistency above 90%" and two national invention patents for its follow-up-questioning/scoring tech (vendor claim, no independent validation found) — [Sohu](https://www.sohu.com/a/977973124_464849); [CSDN](https://www.csdn.net/article/2026-08-21/163940149) (search snippet; pages not opened)
- Chinese press coverage reports candidate complaints (no rejection notice, repetitive tests, candidates able to "mass-produce high-scoring answers") — [光明网 Guangming, Nov 2026](https://m.gmw.cn/2026-11/02/content_1304581828.htm) (search snippet; page not opened)

### Inferences
- The industry trajectory is away from "infer hidden traits from incidental signals" (faces, LinkedIn text) toward (a) scoring answers to structured, job-relevant questions against human-rater rubrics and (b) conventional test items for cognitive ability. A product estimating IQ from unstructured chat logs would be an outlier relative to where even aggressive vendors landed.
- Reported language-vs-criterion correlations in this space are modest (Sapia r ≈ 0.37 vs. self-report personality); HireVue's higher r = 0.69 is against human ratings of the same answers, which is an agreement/reliability metric rather than validity against an independent ability test.
- The Pymetrics 2026 study shows that a vendor's own pooled fairness analysis can pass while per-decision-context analysis fails; any validation of the user's tool should disaggregate.

### Gaps
- Textio and Receptiviti: not researched in this session (Textio analyzes job-post language rather than candidates, to my knowledge; I did not verify Receptiviti's claims). No sources gathered.
- I found no commercial product that publicly claims to output an IQ number from free text, but I cannot rule out small consumer apps; absence of evidence, not a confirmed negative.
- The outcome of the HireVue BIPA class action (Deyerler v. HireVue) was not checked.
- Humantic AI's response to the Rhea et al. audit and its current product claims were not checked.
- Final publication venue of Rhea et al. (beyond arXiv) not verified.

## Q2. EU AI Act: prohibited practices, high-risk categories, current timelines (as of Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Text-only inference of emotions/EQ is not covered by the Article 5(1)(f) workplace/education emotion-recognition ban (in force since 2 Feb 2025), because that ban and the "emotion recognition" high-risk category require biometric data; adding voice, video or keystroke dynamics would change that. Independently of data type, any use for recruitment, worker evaluation, or educational admission/assessment is high-risk under Annex III, with obligations now deferred to 2 December 2027 by the Digital Omnibus (Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).

### Cited Findings
- Article 5(1)(f) prohibits AI systems that infer emotions of natural persons in the workplace and education institutions, except for medical or safety reasons; prohibitions apply from 2 February 2025; the Commission issued (non-binding) guidelines on prohibited practices in February 2025 — [Lewis Silkin, 17 Feb 2025](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011); [Orrick, Apr 2025](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/04/EU-Commission-Publishes-Guidelines-on-the-Prohibited-AI-Practices-under-the-AI-Act)
- The Commission guidelines read "workplace" broadly and extend the ban to job candidates; example of prohibited use: call-centre webcam/voice analysis tracking employees' anger — [Bird & Bird](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/global/ai-and-the-workplace-navigating-prohibited-ai-practices-in-the-eu); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)
- Article 3(39) defines an emotion recognition system as one identifying/inferring emotions or intentions "on the basis of their biometric data"; the guidelines clarify that inferring emotions from written text (e.g., content/sentiment analysis) is outside the prohibition, while behavioural biometrics such as keystrokes or body posture are within it — [Future of Privacy Forum](https://fpf.org/blog/red-lines-under-eu-ai-act-unpacking-the-prohibition-of-emotion-recognition-in-the-workplace-and-education-institutions/); [Thematic analysis](https://getthematic.com/insights/eu-ai-act-emotion-recognition-sentiment-analysis); [Technology's Legal Edge, Apr 2025](https://www.technologyslegaledge.com/2025/04/eu-ai-act-spotlight-on-emotional-recognition-systems-in-the-workplace/)
- Annex III high-risk categories include: 1(b) biometric categorisation according to sensitive/protected attributes and 1(c) emotion recognition (both biometric-based); 3(a)–(d) education (determining access/admission, evaluating learning outcomes, assessing appropriate level of education, monitoring students during tests); 4(a) AI "intended to be used for the recruitment or selection of natural persons" and 4(b) AI used for decisions affecting work relationships, including evaluating performance/behaviour — [AI Act Annex III (artificialintelligenceact.eu)](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/annex/3/)
- Article 6(3): an Annex III system is always considered high-risk where it performs profiling of natural persons (i.e., the "narrow procedural task" carve-out is unavailable for profiling) — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (regulation text, not re-fetched this session)
- Article 5(1)(g) bans biometric categorisation to deduce race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religious/philosophical beliefs, sex life or sexual orientation; Article 5(1)(c) bans social scoring leading to detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts or disproportionate treatment — [Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (regulation text, not re-fetched this session)
- Digital Omnibus on AI = Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, dated 8 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026; Annex III high-risk obligations now apply from 2 December 2027 (previously 2 Aug 2026), Annex I product-embedded from 2 August 2028; adds new Article 5 prohibitions on AI generating non-consensual intimate imagery and CSAM (from 2 Dec 2026); does not change the emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation rules — [AI Act Explorer: Digital Omnibus](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)
- Secondary sources differ slightly on the adoption chronology (EP vote 16 June 2026, Council adoption 29 June 2026, OJ publication 24 July 2026) — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [aiacto.eu](https://www.aiacto.eu/en/blog/digital-omnibus-ai-act-report-2027)
- Member States may adopt stricter national rules on workplace emotion recognition — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/02/17/understanding-the-eu-ai-acts-prohibited-practices-key-workplace-and-advertising-102k011)

### Inferences
- Design boundary: keep the tool text-only if it could ever be used on employees, candidates or students in the EU. Adding voice tone, video, or typing-rhythm features to an "EQ" estimate would plausibly turn it into a prohibited workplace/education emotion-recognition system.
- "EQ" as a stable trait score is arguably not "emotion" inference, but if the system infers momentary emotional states or intentions from biometric signals the ban bites; the trait/state distinction should be documented.
- A personal/consumer "curiosity" tool used on one's own chats is not an Annex III use. The moment it is marketed or used for hiring, promotion, team assessment, admissions or grading it becomes high-risk (risk management, data governance, technical documentation, human oversight, accuracy/robustness, conformity assessment) from 2 Dec 2027.
- Producing IQ scores of third parties that are then used to treat them detrimentally in unrelated contexts edges toward the Article 5(1)(c) social-scoring concern; this is speculative and fact-dependent.

### Gaps
- I did not read the Commission guidelines' primary text (C(2025) 884 or equivalent); paragraph numbers not verified.
- Whether national regulators have issued enforcement actions under Article 5(1)(f) by Nov 2026 was not checked.
- Whether the Digital Omnibus on data (GDPR amendments proposed Nov 2025) has been adopted, and whether it changes profiling/special-category rules, was not checked.

## Q3. GDPR: profiling, Article 22, special-category data, and third parties' messages in a chat export

### Takeaway
Estimating IQ/EQ/personality from messages is "profiling" under GDPR; decisions based solely on it with significant effects are restricted by Article 22; and inferences can become special-category data (e.g., health/disability) because the CJEU reads "revealing" to include inferred data. The other participants in an exported chat are data subjects too: the household exemption likely covers a private individual analyzing their own chats for personal purposes, but a service provider that receives and analyzes uploaded chats is a controller for everyone in them, with transparency (Art. 14) and lawful-basis problems.

### Cited Findings
- Article 4(4) defines profiling as automated processing to evaluate personal aspects, "in particular to analyse or predict aspects concerning that natural person's performance at work, economic situation, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behaviour…"; Article 22(1) gives a right not to be subject to a decision "based solely on automated processing, including profiling," producing legal or similarly significant effects; Article 22(4) restricts such decisions based on special-category data; Article 14 requires informing data subjects when data are obtained from someone other than them; Article 2(2)(c) excludes processing "by a natural person in the course of a purely personal or household activity" — [GDPR, EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj) (regulation text, not re-fetched this session)
- CJEU Grand Chamber, C-184/20 OT v Vyriausioji tarnybinės etikos komisija (1 Aug 2022): data capable of indirectly revealing sensitive information through "an intellectual operation involving deduction or cross-referencing" falls under Article 9 — [Covington Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/eu-data-protection/special-category-data-by-inference-cjeu-significantly-expands-the-scope-of-article-9-gdpr/); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2022/global/what-is-in-a-name-cjeu)
- Household exemption examples given by the Irish DPC are private contact lists and family photo sharing — [Irish DPC FAQ](https://dataprotection.ie/en/faqs/general/what-household-exemption)
- Academic analysis notes that personal records can lose the exemption where they entail acting on the data, engaging persons outside the home/family sphere, or disclosing third parties' data — [van der Sloot, JIPITEC](https://jde.pubsys.hbz-nrw.de/index.php/jipitec/article/download/683/837/6323)
- A UK High Court judgment treated a members-only club chat as within the personal-activity exemption (UK GDPR), showing the line is fact-specific — [Freevacy](https://www.freevacy.com/news/information-rights-and-wrongs/testing-the-limits-of-purely-personal-and-household-use-gdpr-exemption/7578)
- The Irish DPC's WhatsApp decision addressed Article 14 obligations toward non-users whose data a platform processes (indirectly relevant to platforms ingesting third parties' data) — [DPC decision, EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2021-09/dpc_final_decision_redacted_for_issue_to_edpb_01-09-21_en.pdf)

### Inferences
- An inferred low cognitive score may "reveal" health data (e.g., intellectual disability, cognitive decline, neurodivergence), so under C-184/20 an operator should assume Article 9 may be engaged and design for explicit consent or avoid outputs that function as health inferences.
- Practical design for third-party data: (1) score only the consenting user's own messages; (2) strip/hash other participants' messages before sending to an LLM API, or use them only as context without generating any inference about them; (3) never output scores about non-consenting chat partners (a "rate your friend's/partner's/colleague's IQ" feature is the highest-risk configuration); (4) minimise retention; (5) note that sending chats to a third-party LLM API is itself a transfer to a processor (DPA/transfer terms needed).
- If the tool is offered as a service, the household exemption protects the user but not the provider.

### Gaps
- EDPB/WP29 profiling guidelines (WP251rev.01) not fetched this session; their specific examples would strengthen this section.
- No regulator decision found specifically on apps that analyze uploaded WhatsApp/WeChat exports.
- CJEU Article 22 case law (e.g., SCHUFA C-634/21, Dec 2023) not fetched this session.

## Q4. China: PIPL and the 2023 Generative AI Interim Measures

### Takeaway
PIPL (in force 1 Nov 2021) requires consent with "full knowledge" for most processing, "separate consent" plus a mandatory impact assessment for sensitive personal information, and gives individuals the right to an explanation and to refuse decisions made solely by automated decision-making that significantly affect them. The Generative AI Interim Measures (in force 15 Aug 2023) add anti-discrimination duties covering, among others, health, and personal-information minimisation for user inputs. Third-party chat content (e.g., WeChat group exports) has no obvious lawful basis without those people's consent.

### Cited Findings
- PIPL Article 13 lists lawful bases, the first being consent; Article 14 requires consent given "under the precondition of full knowledge" and "in a voluntary and explicit statement," with re-consent when purpose/method changes — [DigiChina translation of PIPL](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-personal-information-protection-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-nov-1-2021/)
- PIPL Article 24 (automated decision-making): handlers must guarantee "the transparency of the decision-making and the fairness and justice of the handling result," may not engage in unreasonable differential treatment; for decisions with major influence on individuals' rights, individuals may demand an explanation and refuse decisions made solely through automated decision-making — [DigiChina](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-personal-information-protection-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-nov-1-2021/)
- PIPL Article 28: sensitive personal information is information that, if leaked or illegally used, "may easily cause harm to the dignity" of persons or their safety/property, including biometric characteristics, religious beliefs, specially-designated status, medical health, financial accounts, location tracking, and minors' (<14) information; may be handled only with a specific purpose and need; Article 29 requires "separate consent"; Article 55 mandates a prior personal information protection impact assessment for sensitive data and for automated decision-making; Article 56 requires reports to be kept at least three years — [DigiChina](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-personal-information-protection-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-nov-1-2021/)
- PIPL entered into force 1 November 2021 (Art. 74) — [DigiChina](https://digichina.stanford.edu/work/translation-personal-information-protection-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-effective-nov-1-2021/)
- Generative AI Interim Measures (CAC + six agencies), effective 15 August 2023, apply to generative AI services offered to the public in mainland China; Article 4 requires effective measures to prevent discrimination (ethnicity, belief, country, region, gender, age, occupation, health, etc.) in algorithm design, training data selection, model generation and service provision, and respect for privacy/personal information rights; Article 11 requires not collecting non-essential personal information and not unlawfully retaining identifiable input/usage records — [China Law Translate](https://www.chinalawtranslate.com/key-changes-to-generative-ai-measures/); [CASI translation (PDF)](https://airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/CASI/documents/Translations/2023-08-07%20ITOW%20Interim%20Measures%20for%20the%20Management%20of%20Generative%20Artificial%20Intelligence%20Services.pdf)
- English translations of the Interim Measures differ in wording; official Chinese text should be checked — [Digital Policy Alert translation](https://clairk-old.digitalpolicyalert.org/documents/china-interim-measures-for-the-management-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-services/raw)

### Inferences
- An IQ/EQ estimate that could reveal mental health or cognitive impairment plausibly falls under "medical health" sensitive information, triggering separate consent + PIPIA (Arts. 28, 29, 55).
- Using such scores for hiring in China would be "automated decision-making" with significant effects (Art. 24): candidates can demand explanation and refuse solely-automated decisions.
- WeChat group exports contain identifiable third-party data; PIPL Art. 13's "publicly disclosed information" basis would not cover private chats, so analyzing others' messages generally needs their consent — and generating inferences about them is the riskier act.
- If the tool is a public-facing generative AI service in China, the Interim Measures' registration/security-assessment and labelling regimes may also apply.

### Gaps
- PIPL Article 72 (exclusion for natural persons handling data for personal or family affairs, the analogue of GDPR's household exemption) not re-verified in this session.
- China's Algorithm Recommendation Provisions (2022), Deep Synthesis Provisions (2023), AI-generated content labelling measures (2025) and employment anti-discrimination law were not researched.
- No Chinese enforcement case found on AI interview/personality inference.

## Q5. United States: EEOC/ADA, disparate impact (Griggs), NYC Local Law 144, Illinois AI Video Interview Act

### Takeaway
Federal AI-specific guidance was withdrawn in January 2025, but Title VII disparate-impact and ADA obligations still apply to any cognitive-ability or personality screen; the cognitive-ability-test lineage starts with Griggs v. Duke Power (1971). State/local rules are the active layer: NYC LL144 bias audits (weakly enforced per a Dec 2025 State Comptroller audit), Illinois' AI Video Interview Act, and Illinois HB 3773 (effective 1 Jan 2026).

### Cited Findings
- The EEOC removed its AI technical-assistance documents (May 2022 ADA document; May 2023 Title VII adverse-impact document) from its website on 27 January 2025 following January 2025 executive orders; these documents were non-binding and the underlying laws are unchanged — [Cooley, Feb 2025](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2025-02-21-gone-but-not-forgotten-federal-laws-still-apply-despite-guidance-disappearance-act); [K&L Gates, Jan 2025](https://www.klgates.com/The-Changing-Landscape-of-AI-Federal-Guidance-for-Employers-Reverses-Course-with-New-Administration-1-31-2025)
- AI systems that screen out candidates on disability-related characteristics may still create ADA liability, and employers remain responsible for reasonable accommodations when using AI — [Husch Blackwell](https://www.huschblackwell.com/newsandinsights/ai-and-workplace-discrimination-what-employers-need-to-know-after-the-eeoc-and-dol-rollbacks)
- States are filling the gap; Illinois HB 3773 (Public Act 103-0804) amends the Illinois Human Rights Act to cover AI in employment decisions, effective 1 January 2026 — [National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/federal-government-quietly-removed-its-ai-hiring-guidance-four-states-are-writing)
- Griggs v. Duke Power Co., 401 U.S. 424 (1971): the Supreme Court held that employment requirements (a high-school diploma and general aptitude/intelligence tests) that disproportionately excluded Black applicants violated Title VII unless shown to be job-related — [Justia](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/401/424/) (case text, not re-fetched this session)
- NYC Local Law 144: NY State Comptroller audit (Report 2024-N-6, released 2 Dec 2025, covering July 2023–June 2025) found DCWP enforcement ineffective; DCWP agreed to adopt most recommendations — [NY OSC audit](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2025/12/02/enforcement-local-law-144-automated-employment-decision-tools); [OSC press release](https://www.osc.ny.gov/press/releases/2025/12/dinapoli-new-yorkers-deserve-transparent-hiring-process-when-artificial-intelligence-used-vet-their); [DLA Piper, Jan 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/01/critical-audit-of-nyc-ai-hiring-law-signals-increased-risk-for-employers)
- Illinois Artificial Intelligence Video Interview Act (820 ILCS 42, effective 1 Jan 2020): employers must notify applicants before the interview that AI may analyze the video, explain how the AI works and what characteristics it evaluates, obtain consent, and delete interviews within 30 days on request; Public Act 102-0047 added a requirement that employers relying solely on AI analysis to select candidates for in-person interviews collect and report race/ethnicity data to the state (DCEO publishes annual reports) — [ILGA 820 ILCS 42/5](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/082000420K5.htm); [Public Act 102-0047](https://witnessslips.ilga.gov/Legislation/publicacts/view/102-0047); [DCEO 2024 AI Video report](https://dceo.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/dceo/aboutdceo/reportsrequiredbystatute/2024-ai-video-report.pdf)

### Inferences
- An "IQ from chat" score used for hiring is functionally a cognitive-ability test; cognitive-ability tests are the classic source of large group differences and thus disparate-impact exposure, so job-relatedness validation (Uniform Guidelines-style) would be needed. Language-based scoring adds a second channel of impact (dialect, non-native English — see Q6).
- ADA risk: a score that tracks language disorders, dyslexia, autism-related communication styles, or cognitive impairment could screen out people with disabilities; accommodation (an alternative assessment) should be designed in.
- The Illinois Act applies to video interviews, so a text-chat tool is likely outside its literal scope, but HB 3773 (Illinois Human Rights Act) and LL144 (any "automated employment decision tool" for NYC jobs) are not limited to video.

## Q6. Ethics and scientific concerns: IQ misuse history, language/dialect bias in LLM scoring, AI-physiognomy critiques, psychometric standards

### Takeaway
There is direct evidence that LLMs judge speakers of African American English more negatively (including as "stupid"/"lazy") and that text-based classifiers misjudge non-native writers; an LLM "IQ from chat" estimator is highly likely to encode these biases unless measured and corrected. The history of IQ testing (eugenics, immigration restriction, forced sterilization) and the "AI physiognomy" critiques make validity and fairness evidence, not just accuracy, the ethical baseline; the AERA/APA/NCME Standards (2014, open access since 2021) provide the professional benchmark.

### Cited Findings
- Hofmann, Kalluri, Jurafsky & King, "AI generates covertly racist decisions about people based on their dialect," Nature 633, 147–154 (2024): language models hold covert stereotypes about AAE speakers more negative than any experimentally recorded human stereotypes about African Americans, while overt stereotypes are positive; models were more likely to assign AAE speakers less-prestigious jobs, convict them, and sentence them to death; human-feedback training did not remove covert bias — [arXiv 2403.00742](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.00742); [Nature News & Views](https://media.nature.com/original/magazine-assets/d41586-024-02527-x/d41586-024-02527-x.pdf)
- Stanford HAI summary: when prompted about AAE speakers, models produced adjectives such as "dirty, stupid, or lazy" — [Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/news/covert-racism-ai-how-language-models-are-reinforcing-outdated-stereotypes); [UChicago News](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/ai-biased-against-speakers-african-american-english-study-finds)
- Liang, Yuksekgonul, Mao, Wu & Zou, "GPT detectors are biased against non-native English writers," Patterns (2023): seven detectors misclassified 61.3% (average) of 91 TOEFL essays by non-native writers as AI-generated, vs. >90% correct on US eighth-grade essays; mechanism is reliance on perplexity (simpler vocabulary = flagged); authors caution against evaluative use on non-native writers — [EurekAlert / Cell Press release](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/994660); [DOI 10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100779)
- Wang & Kosinski's facial "sexual orientation" classifier (JPSP) was criticized by Agüera y Arcas, Todorov and Mitchell as resting on physiognomy and on superficial presentation cues (grooming, glasses, photo angle) from 35,326 dating-site images; a reanalysis found a handful of yes/no questions about self-presentation did nearly as well; critics called it "AI phrenology" — [Agüera y Arcas, Todorov & Mitchell (Medium, archived)](https://gwern.net/docs/www/medium.com/2b3924a875937ddbd65ab929c6d4a39d22ff7e3e.html); [Privacy International](https://www.privacyinternational.org/examples/1936/questions-continue-about-study-claiming-ai-can-infer-sexual-orientation-facial-images); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/opinion-the-stanford-gaydar-ai-is-hogwash)
- IQ misuse history: WWI Army Alpha/Beta results (on over a million recruits; estimates vary, ~1.75M) were used by eugenicists in debates leading to the 1924 Immigration Act; Brigham's 1922 analysis argued immigration and racial mixing lowered American intelligence; in Buck v. Bell (1927) the Supreme Court upheld compulsory sterilization 8–1, and an estimated 60,000–70,000 Americans were sterilized under such laws; critics argued the tests reflected acculturation and testing conditions rather than innate ability — [Understanding Race (AAA)](https://understandingrace.org/history/science/race-and-intelligence-1900-1930/); [Eugenics Archive](https://eugenicsarchive.ca/discover/encyclopedia/535eecb77095aa000000023a); [Encyclopedia.com](https://encyclopedia.com/social-sciences/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/iq-and-testing-origin-and-development). Figures vary across these secondary sources.
- The Standards for Educational and Psychological Testing (AERA/APA/NCME, 2014 edition) became free open access on 1 March 2021; the 2014 revision added a dedicated fairness chapter and threads fairness throughout — [AERA announcement](https://www.aera.net/Newsroom/AERA-APA-and-NCME-Announce-the-Open-Access-Release-of-Standards-for-Educational-and-Psychological-Testing); [testingstandards.net open-access files](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html); [NCME](https://ncme.org/resources/books/testing-standards/)
- Privacy advocates object to personality profiling without the subject's explicit consent — [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/were-doing-ai-phrenology-again-2000553600)

### Inferences
- Writing style in chats is confounded with dialect, native language, education, age, typing device, platform norms, and disability; a model that maps style to "intelligence" can reproduce class/race/language hierarchies even if accuracy on a convenience sample looks good. This is the textual analogue of the physiognomy critique: the model may learn presentation cues correlated with the label in the sample rather than the construct.
- LLM-judged "intelligence" is particularly exposed because the Hofmann results show the bias is covert (not visible when asking about groups explicitly), so prompt instructions like "be unbiased" are not adequate mitigation; it must be measured with matched-guise style tests (same content, different dialect/fluency).
- Publishing a single IQ number to a user (or about someone else) carries labeling/stigma harms disproportionate to the measurement precision achievable (see Q7).

## Q7. Practical validation guidance for an "IQ (and EQ) from chat logs" tool

### Takeaway
A credible study needs an independent, validated ability measure as ground truth (ICAR is public-domain and has ICAR16–WAIS-IV convergence evidence), a sample large and diverse enough to estimate r with narrow CIs and to test fairness across language groups, stability checks (different chat samples/time windows/prompt variants), and reporting of r, MAE/prediction intervals and group-wise calibration rather than point IQ numbers. Prior vendor "validation" in this space (self-report personality, human-rater agreement) would not meet that bar for an IQ claim.

### Cited Findings
- ICAR (International Cognitive Ability Resource) is an international collaboration producing open-source, public-domain cognitive-ability items usable in non-proctored settings; Condon & Revelle (2014, Intelligence) evaluated the first four item types and found public-domain status did not compromise concurrent/discriminant validity in ~97,000 online participants — [Condon & Revelle 2014 (PDF)](https://gwern.net/doc/iq/2014-condon.pdf)
- The initial ICAR item set: 60 items (9 Letter-Number Series, 11 Matrix Reasoning, 16 Verbal Reasoning, 24 Three-Dimensional Rotation), administered to ~97,000 people via SAPA between Aug 2010 and May 2013; validated against self-reported achievement test scores and university majors — [Journal of Open Psychology Data 2016](https://doaj.org/article/e90aa359ea554359931b440c40c0f737)
- ICAR has grown to items covering 19 subdomains — [Dworak, Revelle, Doebler & Condon review (PDF)](https://personality-project.org/revelle/publications/paid.icar.pdf)
- Young & Keith (2020, Journal of Psychoeducational Assessment): in 97 students, ICAR16 correlated with WAIS-IV FSIQ r = .81 and CFA general factors r = .94; authors call ICAR16 a valid brief measure but call for larger replications. Caution: one source suggests the .81 may be corrected for range restriction/reliability — [SAGE (PDF)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/pdf/10.1177/0734282920943455)
- A University of Texas study (N = 67 students + 30 clinical participants) found ICAR tasks correlated lowest with WAIS-IV processing speed and working memory (r = .07–.32) — [UT repository](https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/items/ec3585ea-25c0-45d0-ac07-c7f96687881f) (search snippet; page not opened)
- Stability/reliability auditing framework (input-format, source, and repeated-run stability) and open-source audit library for algorithmic personality tests — [Rhea et al., arXiv 2201.09151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.09151)
- Precedent for disaggregated fairness analysis: per-position four-fifths-rule analysis revealed adverse impact that pooled analysis missed — [Fortune on "Algorithmic Monocultures in Hiring"](https://fortune.com/2026/05/26/ai-hiring-algorithm-racial-disparities-pymetrics-stanford-study/)
- Psychometric benchmark: AERA/APA/NCME Standards (validity, reliability/precision, fairness) — [testingstandards.net](https://www.testingstandards.net/open-access-files.html)
- Examples of weak criteria to avoid relying on alone: language vs. self-report personality (Sapia, r = 0.37) — [Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2022.865841/pdf); language model vs. human raters (HireVue, r = 0.69) — [HireVue](https://learn.hirevue.com/ai-readiness-guide)

### Inferences
(Methodological recommendations derived from the sources above plus standard psychometric arithmetic; not individually sourced.)
- Ground truth: administer a validated ability test to every participant independently of the chats, ideally proctored or at least timed and attention-checked. ICAR (e.g., ICAR16 or a longer ICAR form) is the practical free option; a subsample with a clinician-administered test (WAIS-IV/5, or Raven's) would anchor the ICAR scale. Note ICAR16 is largely non-verbal (and weakly related to processing speed/working memory), so a text model may align differently with verbal vs. fluid ability; report against both a verbal and a non-verbal criterion if possible.
- Precision arithmetic to communicate to users: with IQ SD = 15, the standard error of estimate is 15·√(1−r²) ≈ 14.3 points at r = 0.30, 13.0 at r = 0.50, 10.7 at r = 0.70, and still 9.0 at r = 0.80. A 95% prediction interval at r = 0.5 is roughly ±25 IQ points — so a point IQ estimate is not defensible; report ranges, percentile bands or "low confidence" outputs.
- Sample size: to estimate r ≈ 0.3–0.5 with a 95% CI of about ±0.05 needs on the order of 1,000+ participants; fairness subgroup analyses need several hundred per group (e.g., native vs. non-native writers, dialect groups, age bands, gender, education levels).
- Report: Pearson/Spearman r with CIs on a held-out (ideally preregistered, out-of-sample, different-population) set; MAE and RMSE in IQ points; calibration plots; group-wise mean error (bias), group-wise r and MAE; differential prediction (regression slopes/intercepts by group, Cleary model); and incremental validity over trivial baselines (message length, vocabulary size, education self-report, demographics) — if a few surface features do nearly as well, the model is "reading presentation," as in the Kosinski critiques.
- Measurement invariance: test whether the text-derived score relates to the criterion the same way across groups (multi-group regression or, if multiple text-derived facets, MGCFA for configural/metric/scalar invariance).
- Reliability/stability: test–retest across different chat samples from the same person (different weeks, different conversation partners, different topics), across LLM runs (temperature, seeds), across prompt paraphrases and model versions, and across input formatting (the Crystal PDF-vs-text failure mode). Report ICCs.
- Matched-guise bias test: rewrite identical content into dialect variants, non-native-like English, different punctuation/emoji/typing styles, and check score shifts (following Hofmann et al.'s design).
- Confounds specific to chat logs: topic and context (work vs. family chats), audience design, code-switching, voice-to-text, autocorrect, copy-pasted or AI-generated text, message length; restrict or control for these.
- EQ: no single gold standard; ability EI tests (e.g., MSCEIT) are proprietary, and trait EI questionnaires are self-report — validation against self-report alone repeats the Sapia limitation. Other researchers cover EQ validity.
- Ethics/process: IRB/ethics approval, informed consent from participants for both test and chat data, consent or exclusion of chat partners' messages, data minimisation, no output about non-consenting third parties, opt-out and deletion, and a clear statement of intended use (personal reflection vs. any high-stakes use).
